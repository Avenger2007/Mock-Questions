In corrosion, at a particular spot of an iron object oxidation takes place and that spot behaves as anode: 2Fe(s) → 2Fe²⁺ + 4e⁻; electrons released at the anodic spot move through the metal to another spot and reduce oxygen in the presence of H⁺ (which is believed to be available from H₂CO₃ formed by dissolution of CO₂ in water): O₂(g) + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O(l). The ferrous ions are further oxidised by atmospheric oxygen to hydrated ferric oxide (rust).
Why: A cell's voltage changes during use only if the concentrations in its Nernst equation change; corrosion needs an electrolyte to complete the circuit between anodic and cathodic regions, so salts and acids accelerate it.
Why not the others: (A) The dry cell gives about 1.5 V and its voltage falls; rusting needs water. (B) The lead cell gives about 2 V, and the acid density falls on discharge. (C) Voltages differ, and salt water speeds rusting.
Key point: Mercury cell 1.35 V (constant), dry cell 1.5 V, lead cell 2 V; rusting is an electrochemical process needing water and oxygen.
