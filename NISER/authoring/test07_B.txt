sub_topic: Counting structures typical of plant cells but not animal cells, and the reverse
Q: Consider these cell structures: cell wall, plastids, centrioles, large central vacuole, mitochondria, Golgi apparatus and ribosomes. How many of them are typically present in a mature cell of a flowering plant but absent from an animal cell, and how many are typically present in an animal cell but absent from a flowering-plant cell?
A) 2 and 2
B) 4 and 1
C) 3 and 1
D) 3 and 0
KEY: C
SOL:
Answer: (C) 3 and 1.
Concept: Plant and animal cells are both eukaryotic and share the membrane-bound organelles (mitochondria, Golgi apparatus, ER) and ribosomes. Plant cells additionally have a cellulose cell wall, plastids and a large central vacuole; animal cells have centrioles, which are absent from the cells of higher plants.
Given: seven structures to classify.
Formula/Principle: count separately the structures unique to each cell type; shared structures count for neither.
Step 1: Plant only: cell wall, plastids, large central vacuole = 3.
Step 2: Animal only: centrioles = 1. Shared: mitochondria, Golgi apparatus, ribosomes.
Why: The cell wall gives shape and support, plastids carry out photosynthesis or storage, and the vacuole keeps turgor. Centrioles organise the spindle in animal cells; higher plant cells form a spindle without them.
Why not the others: (A) Treats the large central vacuole as common to both; animal cells have only small vacuoles. (B) Counts one of the shared organelles as plant-only. (D) Misses centrioles, which are found in animal cells but not in flowering-plant cells.
Key point: Plant-only: wall, plastids, large vacuole; animal-only: centrioles.

B) Lipids are polymers of fatty acids joined by peptide bonds

Answer: (A) Triglyceride: glycerol + three fatty acids; phospholipid: glycerol + two fatty acids + phosphorylated group (polar head); unsaturated fatty acids have C=C bonds and tend to form oils.

A) The cardiac cycle stays 0.8 s long; only the stroke volume changes
B) The first heart sound ("lub") is caused by closure of the semilunar valves at the end of ventricular systole
C) The cycle shortens to 0.5 s, and most of the shortening comes from diastole, which falls from 0.5 s (0.1 + 0.4) to about 0.25 s
D) The cycle shortens to 0.5 s, and the shortening comes mostly from ventricular systole
KEY: C
SOL:
Answer: (C) The cycle shortens to 0.5 s, mostly by shortening diastole.
Concept: The cardiac cycle is the sequence of events in one beat. At 72–75 beats per minute it lasts about 0.8 s. The first heart sound ("lub") is due to closure of the atrioventricular (tricuspid and bicuspid) valves; the second ("dub") is due to closure of the semilunar valves.
Given: 75 min⁻¹ at rest; 120 min⁻¹ in exercise; ventricular systole 0.3 s → 0.25 s.
Formula/Principle: cycle duration = 60 s ÷ heart rate; ventricular diastole = cycle − ventricular systole.
Step 1: At rest: 60/75 = 0.8 s; ventricular diastole = 0.8 − 0.3 = 0.5 s (0.1 s atrial systole + 0.4 s joint diastole).
Step 2: In exercise: 60/120 = 0.5 s; ventricular diastole = 0.5 − 0.25 = 0.25 s.
Why: The cycle loses 0.3 s; systole loses only 0.05 s, so diastole loses 0.25 s. Diastole, when the ventricles fill, is the phase that is cut most at high heart rates.
Why not the others: (A) A heart rate of 120 per minute means each cycle lasts 60/120 = 0.5 s. (B) "Lub" is closure of the AV valves at the start of ventricular systole; "dub" is closure of the semilunar valves. (D) Systole changed by only 0.05 s.
Key point: Cycle time = 60 s ÷ heart rate; rising heart rate mainly shortens diastole.

Q: A population of 100 individuals has an intrinsic rate of natural increase r = 0.10 per year. Compare its size after 20 years (a) if growth is exponential, and (b) if growth is logistic with a carrying capacity K = 500. (e² ≈ 7.39)
A) (a) about 300; (b) about 300
B) (a) about 740; (b) 500
C) (a) about 740; (b) about 740
D) (a) about 740; (b) about 320, still below K and growing more slowly
KEY: D
SOL:
Answer: (D) (a) about 740; (b) about 320.
Concept: When resources are unlimited, growth is exponential: dN/dt = rN, so Nₜ = N₀e^(rt) (J-shaped curve). In nature resources are limited, so growth follows the logistic (Verhulst–Pearl) model: dN/dt = rN(K − N)/K, giving a sigmoid curve that levels off at K.
Given: N₀ = 100; r = 0.10 yr⁻¹; t = 20 yr; K = 500.
Formula/Principle: exponential Nₜ = N₀e^(rt); logistic Nₜ = K / [1 + ((K − N₀)/N₀) e^(−rt)].
Step 1: Exponential: Nₜ = 100 × e² ≈ 739.
Step 2: Logistic: Nₜ = 500 / (1 + 4e⁻²) = 500 / (1 + 4 × 0.135) = 500 / 1.54 ≈ 324.
Why: In the logistic model the factor (K − N)/K slows growth as N rises, so the population stays below K. The exponential population is not limited and has already exceeded the carrying capacity.
Why not the others: (A) Uses linear growth (100 + 100 × 0.10 × 20). (B) The logistic population approaches K only gradually; after 20 years it is still well below 500. (C) Ignores the limiting effect of K in the logistic model.
Key point: Exponential: N₀e^(rt); logistic adds (K − N)/K, slowing growth well before K is reached.
