2,4-Dibromophenol | 252

What mass of precipitate forms, and how many moles of \(\ce{Br2}\) are consumed?
A) 1.73 g of 4-bromophenol; 0.010 mol
B) 3.31 g of 2,4,6-tribromophenol; 0.015 mol
C) 2.52 g of 2,4-dibromophenol; 0.020 mol
D) 3.31 g of 2,4,6-tribromophenol; 0.030 mol
KEY: D
SOL:
Answer: (D) 3.31 g of 2,4,6-tribromophenol; 0.030 mol.
Concept: The –OH group strongly activates the benzene ring and directs to the ortho and para positions. In water (a polar solvent), phenol reacts with bromine to give 2,4,6-tribromophenol, a white precipitate. In a non-polar solvent such as CS₂ at low temperature, mainly monobromophenols form.
Why: \(\ce{C6H5OH + 3Br2 -> C6H2Br3OH + 3HBr}\). Phenol = 0.94/94 = 0.010 mol, which gives 0.010 mol of tribromophenol = 0.010 × 331 = 3.31 g. Each substitution uses one Br₂, so 3 × 0.010 = 0.030 mol of Br₂ is consumed.
Why not the others: (A) Monobromination occurs in CS₂ at low temperature, not with excess bromine water. (B) It counts half the bromine: each Br₂ supplies only one Br atom to the ring; the other forms HBr. (C) With excess bromine water all three activated positions (2, 4 and 6) are substituted.
Key point: Phenol + bromine water → 2,4,6-tribromophenol (white ppt) using 3 Br₂ per phenol.

C) S < Q < P < R

SOL:
Answer: (D) Q < S < P < R.

Step 1: Bracket values: Q (2→1) 3/4 = 0.750; S (4→2) 3/16 = 0.1875; P (3→2) 5/36 = 0.139; R (4→3) 7/144 = 0.0486.
Step 2: Wavelengths: Q 122 nm, S 486 nm, P 656 nm, R 1875 nm.
Why: The largest bracket gives the shortest wavelength, so Q < S < P < R. Lyman lines are in the ultraviolet, Balmer lines in the visible and Paschen lines in the infrared.
Why not the others: (A) This is the decreasing order of wavelength (the order of increasing energy reversed). (B) Balmer β (4→2) has a larger energy gap than Balmer α (3→2), so its wavelength is shorter. (C) Lyman α has the largest energy gap of the four and the shortest wavelength.

sub_topic: Total number of lone pairs on the central atoms of five molecules (VSEPR)
Q: What is the total number of lone pairs on the central atoms of \(\ce{ClF3}\), \(\ce{SF4}\), \(\ce{XeF2}\), \(\ce{IF5}\) and \(\ce{NH3}\), taken together?
A) 7
B) 8
C) 9
D) 6
KEY: B
SOL:
Answer: (B) 8.
Concept: In VSEPR theory, the number of lone pairs on the central atom = (valence electrons of the central atom − electrons used in bonds) ÷ 2. For single bonds to F or H, each bond uses one electron of the central atom.
Given: valence electrons: Cl 7, S 6, Xe 8, I 7, N 5; bonds: 3, 4, 2, 5, 3.
Formula/Principle: lone pairs = (V − number of single bonds) ÷ 2.
Step 1: ClF₃: (7 − 3)/2 = 2; SF₄: (6 − 4)/2 = 1; XeF₂: (8 − 2)/2 = 3.
Step 2: IF₅: (7 − 5)/2 = 1; NH₃: (5 − 3)/2 = 1. Total = 2 + 1 + 3 + 1 + 1 = 8.
Why: The shapes follow: ClF₃ T-shaped, SF₄ see-saw, XeF₂ linear, IF₅ square pyramidal and NH₃ trigonal pyramidal.
Why not the others: (A) 7 comes from taking two lone pairs on Xe in XeF₂ (that is XeF₄). (C) 9 counts two lone pairs on S in SF₄. (D) 6 misses the lone pairs on N and I.
Key point: Lone pairs on the central atom = (valence electrons − bonding electrons it supplies) ÷ 2.
