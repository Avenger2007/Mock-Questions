Also, because transition metal ions can change their oxidation states, they become more effective as catalysts; for example, iron(III) catalyses the reaction between iodide and persulphate ions: 2I⁻ + S₂O₈²⁻ → I₂ + 2SO₄²⁻, via 2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂ and 2Fe²⁺ + S₂O₈²⁻ → 2Fe³⁺ + 2SO₄²⁻. Interstitial compounds are those formed when small atoms like H, C or N are trapped inside the crystal lattices of metals; they are usually non-stoichiometric and are neither typically ionic nor covalent (e.g. TiC, Mn₄N, Fe₃H, VH₀.₅₆, TiH₁.₇); they have high melting points, higher than those of the pure metals, are very hard (some borides approach diamond in hardness), retain metallic conductivity and are chemically inert. An alloy is a blend of metals prepared by mixing the components; alloys may be homogeneous solid solutions in which the atoms of one metal are distributed randomly among the atoms of the other; such alloys are formed by atoms with metallic radii within about 15 per cent of each other; because of similar radii and other characteristics of transition metals, alloys are readily formed by these metals.
Why: The direct reaction between two anions is slow; Fe³⁺ splits it into two fast steps between ions of opposite charge and is regenerated.
Why not the others: (A) Catalysts lower the activation energy. (B) A catalyst is regenerated, not consumed. (C) Interstitial compounds are hard, high-melting and conducting.
Key point: Variable oxidation states and surface adsorption make transition metals good catalysts; interstitial compounds are hard and conducting.

Q: Which valence bond description is correct?
A) [NiCl₄]²⁻ is dsp² and diamagnetic, and [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ is sp³ and paramagnetic
B) [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ is square planar (dsp²) and diamagnetic, because the strong-field CN⁻ ligands pair the eight 3d electrons into four orbitals; [NiCl₄]²⁻ is tetrahedral (sp³) and paramagnetic with two unpaired electrons; [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ is an inner-orbital octahedral complex (d²sp³) and diamagnetic, while [CoF₆]³⁻ is an outer-orbital octahedral complex (sp³d²) and paramagnetic with four unpaired electrons
C) [CoF₆]³⁻ is diamagnetic because F⁻ is a strong-field ligand that pairs electrons
D) Hybridisation of the metal is always the same in complexes of the same oxidation state, whatever the ligand
KEY: B
SOL:
Answer: (B) [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ dsp² diamagnetic; [NiCl₄]²⁻ sp³, 2 unpaired; [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ d²sp³ diamagnetic; [CoF₆]³⁻ sp³d², 4 unpaired.
Concept: According to valence bond theory, the metal atom or ion under the influence of ligands can use its (n − 1)d, ns, np or ns, np, nd orbitals for hybridisation to yield a set of equivalent orbitals of definite geometry: sp³ (tetrahedral), dsp² (square planar), sp³d² or d²sp³ (octahedral). These hybridised orbitals overlap with ligand orbitals that can donate electron pairs. Ni²⁺ is 3d⁸: in [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ the strong CN⁻ ligands force the 3d electrons to pair, freeing one 3d orbital, so the hybridisation is dsp², the geometry square planar and the complex diamagnetic; in [NiCl₄]²⁻, Cl⁻ is a weak-field ligand, the two unpaired 3d electrons remain, the hybridisation is sp³, the geometry tetrahedral, and the complex is paramagnetic. Co³⁺ is 3d⁶: in [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ the six electrons pair into three 3d orbitals, leaving two inner d orbitals for d²sp³ hybridisation; such a complex, using inner (n − 1)d orbitals, is called an inner-orbital or low-spin complex and is diamagnetic; in [CoF₆]³⁻ the outer 4d orbitals are used (sp³d²), forming an outer-orbital or high-spin complex with four unpaired electrons, which is paramagnetic. A limitation of VBT is that it does not distinguish between weak and strong ligands on its own; this must be taken from magnetic data.
Given: Ni²⁺ 3d⁸; Co³⁺ 3d⁶.
Formula/Principle: Strong-field ligand ⇒ pairing ⇒ inner d orbitals used; weak-field ⇒ no pairing ⇒ outer orbitals or sp³.
Step 1: [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻: 3d⁸ paired into 4 orbitals ⇒ dsp², 0 unpaired.
Step 2: [NiCl₄]²⁻: 2 unpaired ⇒ sp³.
Step 3: [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺: d²sp³, 0 unpaired; [CoF₆]³⁻: sp³d², 4 unpaired.
Why: Whether d electrons pair decides which orbitals are free for hybridisation, and that fixes both geometry and magnetism.
Why not the others: (A) Descriptions swapped between the two nickel complexes. (C) F⁻ is weak-field; [CoF₆]³⁻ is paramagnetic. (D) Hybridisation depends on the ligand.
Key point: CN⁻/NH₃ pair electrons (dsp², d²sp³, diamagnetic); Cl⁻/F⁻ do not (sp³, sp³d², paramagnetic).
