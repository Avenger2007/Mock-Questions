Leaf Q shows vascular bundles of nearly similar size (except in the main veins), red dye again in the xylem towards the upper side, and mesophyll not differentiated into palisade and spongy tissue. Which interpretation is correct?
A) P is a monocot leaf, because its bundles differ in size; the dye moves through the phloem
B) Both leaves are isobilateral, because the dye reaches both of them in the same way
C) Q is a dicot leaf with reticulate venation, which is why its bundles are similar in size
D) P is a dorsiventral (dicot) leaf whose reticulate venation gives bundles of varying sizes, and Q is an isobilateral (monocot) leaf whose parallel venation gives bundles of similar size; in both, water (and the dye) moves up through the xylem, which lies towards the upper (adaxial) side with phloem towards the lower side, and each bundle is surrounded by a layer of bundle sheath cells
KEY: D
SOL:
Answer: (D) P is dorsiventral (dicot), Q isobilateral (monocot); xylem is adaxial and carries the dye in both.
Concept: A vertical section of a dorsiventral (dicot) leaf shows three main parts: epidermis, mesophyll and vascular system. Why: Eosin travels with water in the xylem, so its position marks the xylem in each bundle; in leaves the xylem faces the upper surface and the phloem the lower. The size pattern of the bundles reflects venation: a branching network gives veins of many sizes, while parallel veins are similar.
Why not the others: (A) Varying bundle size and palisade tissue mark a dicot, and water moves in xylem, not phloem. (B) Only Q lacks palisade/spongy differentiation; P is dorsiventral. (C) Similar-sized bundles reflect parallel venation of a monocot.
Key point: Dicot leaf: palisade + spongy, bundles of varied size; monocot leaf: undifferentiated mesophyll, bundles of similar size; xylem adaxial in both.
