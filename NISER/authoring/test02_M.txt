Q: Let \(f(x)=(x-1)\,|x^2-3x+2|\) for all real x. Where is f not differentiable?
A) Nowhere
B) Only at \(x=2\)
C) At \(x=1\) and \(x=2\)
D) Only at \(x=1\)
KEY: B
SOL:
Answer: (B) Only at \(x=2\).
Given: \(f(x)=(x-1)|x^2-3x+2|=(x-1)|x-1|\,|x-2|\)
Formula/Principle: \(g(x)=(x-a)|x-a|\ \text{is differentiable at}\ a;\ |x-b|\ \text{has a corner at}\ b\)
Step 1: \(\text{near}\ x=1:\ f=(x-1)|x-1|\cdot|x-2|,\ \text{product of differentiable functions}\)
Step 2: \(\text{near}\ x=2:\ f(x)=h(x)|x-2|,\ h(x)=(x-1)|x-1|,\ h(2)=1\ne0\)
Step 3: \(f'(2^+)=h(2)=1,\quad f'(2^-)=-h(2)=-1\)
Worked derivation: \[f'(1)=\lim_{x\to1}\frac{(x-1)|x-1||x-2|}{x-1}=\lim_{x\to1}|x-1||x-2|=0;\qquad f'(2^\pm)=\pm1\]
Check: \[f\ \text{is continuous everywhere, so a corner, not a jump, occurs at}\ x=2\]
Why: \(\text{a factor that vanishes at the corner smooths it; a non-zero factor keeps it}\)
Why not the others: (A) \(f'(2^+)\ne f'(2^-)\); (C) \(f'(1)=0\ \text{exists}\); (D) \(x=1\ \text{is smooth and}\ x=2\ \text{is the corner}\).
Key point: \((x-a)|x-a|\ \text{is differentiable;}\ c\,|x-a|\ (c\ne0)\ \text{is not}\)

Q: For \(|x|<1\), let \(y=\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{2x}{1+x^2}\right)\). What is \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\)?
A) \(\dfrac2{1+x^2}\)
B) \(\dfrac1{1+x^2}\)
C) \(-\dfrac2{1+x^2}\)
D) \(\dfrac2{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\)
KEY: A
SOL:
Answer: (A) \(\dfrac2{1+x^2}\).
Given: \(y=\sin^{-1}\dfrac{2x}{1+x^2},\quad|x|<1\)
Formula/Principle: \(x=\tan\theta\Rightarrow\dfrac{2\tan\theta}{1+\tan^2\theta}=\sin2\theta\)
Step 1: \(|x|<1\Rightarrow\theta\in\left(-\tfrac\pi4,\tfrac\pi4\right)\Rightarrow2\theta\in\left(-\tfrac\pi2,\tfrac\pi2\right)\)
Step 2: \(y=\sin^{-1}(\sin2\theta)=2\theta=2\tan^{-1}x\)
Worked derivation: \[\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{d}{dx}\left(2\tan^{-1}x\right)=\frac2{1+x^2}\]
Check: \[x=0:\ y\approx\sin^{-1}(2x)\approx2x\Rightarrow y'(0)=2=\frac2{1+0}\]
Why: \(2\theta\ \text{lies in the principal range of}\ \sin^{-1}\ \text{only when}\ |x|<1\)
Why not the others: (B) \(\text{drops the factor 2 from}\ 2\tan^{-1}x\); (C) \(\text{that is the derivative for}\ |x|>1\); (D) \(\text{differentiates as if}\ y=2\sin^{-1}x\).
Key point: \(\sin^{-1}\dfrac{2x}{1+x^2}=2\tan^{-1}x\ \text{for}\ |x|\le1\)

Q: On which intervals is \(f(x)=x^2e^{-x}\) increasing, and on which is it decreasing?
A) Increasing on \((-\infty,0]\); decreasing on \([0,2]\)
B) Increasing on \([0,\infty)\)
C) Increasing on \([2,\infty)\); decreasing on \([0,2]\)
D) Increasing on \([0,2]\); decreasing on \((-\infty,0]\) and on \([2,\infty)\)
KEY: D
SOL:
Answer: (D) Increasing on \([0,2]\); decreasing on \((-\infty,0]\) and on \([2,\infty)\).
Given: \(f(x)=x^2e^{-x}\)
Formula/Principle: \(f'>0\Rightarrow\text{increasing};\ f'<0\Rightarrow\text{decreasing}\)
Step 1: \(f'(x)=2xe^{-x}-x^2e^{-x}=x(2-x)e^{-x}\)
Step 2: \(e^{-x}>0;\ \text{sign of}\ f'=\text{sign of}\ x(2-x)\)
Step 3: \(x<0:\ f'<0;\quad0<x<2:\ f'>0;\quad x>2:\ f'<0\)
Worked derivation: \[f'(x)=x(2-x)e^{-x}\ \Rightarrow\ \uparrow\ \text{on}\ [0,2],\ \downarrow\ \text{on}\ (-\infty,0]\cup[2,\infty)\]
Check: \[f(0)=0,\ f(2)=4e^{-2}\approx0.54,\ f(4)=16e^{-4}\approx0.29:\ \text{rises then falls}\]
Why: \(\text{the factor}\ e^{-x}\ \text{eventually beats}\ x^2\)
Why not the others: (A) \(f'(-1)=-3e<0\ \text{on}\ (-\infty,0)\); (B) \(f'(3)=-3e^{-3}<0\); (C) \(\text{reverses the signs of}\ f'\).
Key point: \(\text{factor}\ f'\ \text{fully and read its sign on each interval}\)

Q: Let \(I_n=\displaystyle\int_0^1x^ne^x\,dx\) for \(n\ge0\). Integration by parts gives \(I_n=e-nI_{n-1}\) for \(n\ge1\). What is \(I_3\)?
A) \(2e-6\)
B) \(e-2\)
C) \(6-2e\)
D) \(e-3\)
KEY: C
SOL:
Answer: (C) \(6-2e\).
Given: \(I_n=\displaystyle\int_0^1x^ne^x\,dx,\quad I_n=e-nI_{n-1}\)
Formula/Principle: \(\displaystyle\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du\ \text{with}\ u=x^n,\ dv=e^xdx\)
Step 1: \(I_0=\displaystyle\int_0^1e^x\,dx=e-1\)
Step 2: \(I_1=e-1\cdot I_0=e-(e-1)=1\)
Step 3: \(I_2=e-2I_1=e-2\)
Worked derivation: \[I_3=e-3I_2=e-3(e-2)=6-2e\]
Check: \[6-2e\approx0.563;\ \ 0<x^3e^x<e\ \text{on}\ (0,1)\ \text{and}\ \int_0^1x^3e\,dx=\tfrac e4\approx0.68>0.563>0\]
Why: \(\left[x^ne^x\right]_0^1=e\ \text{for}\ n\ge1\)
Why not the others: (A) \(\text{sign error:}\ 2e-6<0,\ \text{but}\ I_3>0\); (B) \(e-2=I_2\); (D) \(\text{uses}\ I_2=1\ \text{in place of}\ e-2\).
Key point: \(I_n=e-nI_{n-1},\ I_0=e-1\)
