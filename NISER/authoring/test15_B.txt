sub_topic: Mapping restriction sites on a circular plasmid from single and double digests, and the roles of sticky ends and DNA ligase
Q: A 5.0 kb circular plasmid is digested. EcoRI alone gives bands of 3.0 kb and 2.0 kb; BamHI alone gives one band of 5.0 kb; EcoRI + BamHI together give bands of 2.5, 2.0 and 0.5 kb. Which interpretation is correct?
A) EcoRI cuts once and BamHI twice; the 0.5 kb band is undigested plasmid
B) EcoRI cuts at two sites and BamHI at one site, which lies inside the 3.0 kb EcoRI fragment, 0.5 kb from one EcoRI site; each enzyme cuts at a specific palindromic sequence, leaving single-stranded sticky ends that can pair with complementary ends cut by the same enzyme, and DNA ligase then joins the sugar–phosphate backbones
C) BamHI does not cut the plasmid, because only one band is seen
D) Both enzymes cut at the same site; ligase is needed to cut the DNA
KEY: B
SOL:
Answer: (B) Two EcoRI sites, one BamHI site inside the 3.0 kb fragment; sticky ends and ligase join DNA.
Given: Circular 5.0 kb plasmid. EcoRI: 3.0 + 2.0 kb; BamHI: 5.0 kb; double digest: 2.5 + 2.0 + 0.5 kb.
Formula/Principle: Cutting a circle at n sites gives n fragments; fragment sizes add up to the plasmid size.
Step 1: EcoRI gives 2 fragments → 2 sites. BamHI gives 1 band of full size → 1 site (a circle cut once becomes one linear molecule).
Step 2: In the double digest the 2.0 kb EcoRI fragment survives intact; the 3.0 kb fragment is split into 2.5 + 0.5 kb, so the BamHI site lies in it, 0.5 kb from one EcoRI site.
Step 3: Check: 2.5 + 2.0 + 0.5 = 5.0 kb.
Concept: Restriction endonucleases are molecular scissors; each recognises a specific palindromic nucleotide sequence in the DNA and cuts each of the two strands a little away from the centre of the palindrome, but between the same two bases on the opposite strands. This leaves single-stranded portions at the ends, which are overhanging stretches called sticky ends. These form hydrogen bonds with their complementary cut counterparts, and this stickiness facilitates the action of the enzyme DNA ligase. The fragments are separated by agarose gel electrophoresis; smaller fragments move farther.
Why: Comparing single and double digests shows which single-digest fragment contains the second enzyme's site.
Why not the others: (A) EcoRI gives two fragments, so it cuts twice. (C) A single full-length band from a circular plasmid means one cut. (D) The digests differ, so the sites differ; ligase joins, not cuts.
Key point: Circular DNA: n cuts → n fragments; a double digest locates one enzyme's site within the other's fragments.
