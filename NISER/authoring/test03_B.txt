sub_topic: Polygenic inheritance with three additive genes: number of phenotypic classes and frequency of the middle class
Q: Skin colour in a model organism is controlled by three unlinked genes A, B and C. Each capital allele adds one equal unit of pigment, and small alleles add none. Two individuals with genotype AaBbCc are crossed. How many phenotypic classes appear among the offspring, and what fraction of the offspring has exactly three capital alleles?
A) 8 classes; 1/8
B) 27 classes; 1/64
C) 7 classes; 1/64
D) 7 classes; 5/16
KEY: D
SOL:
Answer: (D) 7 classes; 5/16.
Concept: In polygenic inheritance, a trait is controlled by several genes, and the phenotype reflects the total contribution of all the alleles (additive effect). Individuals with the same number of capital alleles have the same phenotype, whatever the genes carrying them.
Given: parents AaBbCc × AaBbCc; three unlinked genes; each capital allele adds one unit.
Formula/Principle: each offspring receives 6 alleles, each capital with probability 1/2; P(k capital alleles) = C(6, k)/2⁶.
Step 1: The number of capital alleles can be 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6, so there are 7 phenotypic classes.
Step 2: P(3 capital alleles) = C(6, 3)/64 = 20/64 = 5/16.
Why: The classes follow the binomial coefficients 1 : 6 : 15 : 20 : 15 : 6 : 1 out of 64. The middle class (three capital alleles, the same as the parents) is the commonest, which gives the bell-shaped distribution typical of polygenic traits.
Why not the others: (A) 8 counts the combinations of three genes as dominant or recessive (2³), which applies only with complete dominance; 1/8 is not any class frequency. (B) 27 is the number of genotypes (3³), not phenotypes, and 1/64 is the frequency of an extreme class. (C) The number of classes is right, but 1/64 is the frequency of the darkest (or lightest) class only.
Key point: n additive genes → 2n + 1 phenotypic classes in the ratio of binomial coefficients.

sub_topic: Time for replication of the E. coli chromosome with two forks, and number of Okazaki fragments
Q: The chromosome of Escherichia coli has \(4.6\times10^6\) base pairs and one origin of replication. Replication proceeds in both directions from the origin, and each replication fork moves at 1000 nucleotides per second along each template. On the lagging strand, DNA is made as Okazaki fragments of about 1000 nucleotides each. About how long does one round of replication take, and about how many Okazaki fragments are made in all?
A) About 77 minutes; about 4600 fragments
B) About 38 minutes; about 4600 fragments
C) About 38 minutes; about 9200 fragments
D) About 19 minutes; about 2300 fragments
KEY: B
SOL:
Answer: (B) About 38 minutes; about 4600 fragments.
Concept: Replication starts at the origin and forms a replication fork moving in each direction. DNA polymerase works only in the 5′ → 3′ direction, so at each fork one strand is made continuously (leading strand) and the other discontinuously as short Okazaki fragments (lagging strand), which are joined by DNA ligase.
Given: genome = 4.6 × 10⁶ bp; 2 forks; 1000 nucleotides per second per fork; Okazaki fragment ≈ 1000 nucleotides.
Formula/Principle: each fork copies half of the chromosome; time = (length per fork) ÷ (rate); lagging-strand length = whole genome (half at each fork).
Step 1: Each fork copies 2.3 × 10⁶ bp, so time = 2.3 × 10⁶ ÷ 1000 = 2300 s ≈ 38 minutes.
Step 2: At each fork the lagging strand is 2.3 × 10⁶ nucleotides, giving 2300 fragments; two forks give about 4600 fragments.
Why: The two forks work at the same time, so they share the chromosome. Only one of the two new strands at each fork is lagging, so the total length made as fragments equals one genome length.
Why not the others: (A) 77 minutes assumes a single fork copying the whole chromosome. (C) 9200 counts both new strands as discontinuous; the leading strand is made continuously. (D) 19 minutes takes 2000 nucleotides per second per fork, and 2300 counts the fragments at one fork only.
Key point: Bidirectional replication halves the time; only the lagging strand is made as Okazaki fragments.

Key point: Mutualism +/+; commensalism +/0; parasitism +/−.

A) About equal, because both receive about the same sunlight per unit area
B) The ocean is about twice as productive per unit area, because water is never limiting there

Answer: (D) Land is about 5 times as productive per unit area, mainly because productivity in the open ocean is limited by low supply of mineral nutrients and by light reaching only the surface layer.

Given: total NPP = 170; ocean NPP = 55, so land NPP = 115 (billion tonnes per year); ocean area = 70%, land area = 30%.

Key point: Ocean NPP is low per unit area because of nutrient limitation, despite its large area.

Concept: Bile released into the duodenum contains bile pigments, bile salts, cholesterol and phospholipids, but no enzymes. Bile helps in emulsification of fats, breaking them into very small micelles, and also activates lipases. Pancreatic lipase then breaks fats into fatty acids and glycerol.
Why: With the duct blocked, fat stays in large globules. Lipase can act only at the surface of a droplet, so digestion is slow, and the undigested fat is passed out in the faeces.
Why not the others: (A) Bile has no digestive enzymes; lipase comes from the pancreas and the intestine. (B) Trypsinogen is activated by enterokinase, and trypsin digests proteins, not fats. (C) Gastric secretion does not depend on bile.

Q: The table compares three muscle tissues.

Tissue | Striations | Control | Cell shape and nuclei | Special feature
P | present | voluntary | long, unbranched, many nuclei | attached to bones
Q | present | involuntary | branched, usually one nucleus | cells joined by intercalated discs

B) P cardiac, Q skeletal, R smooth
C) P skeletal, Q smooth, R cardiac
D) P smooth, Q cardiac, R skeletal
KEY: A
SOL:
Answer: (A) P skeletal, Q cardiac, R smooth.
Concept: Skeletal muscle is striated, voluntary and made of long multinucleate fibres attached to bones. Cardiac muscle is striated and involuntary; its branched cells are joined end to end by intercalated discs, which let them contract as a unit. Smooth (visceral) muscle has no striations, is involuntary and has spindle-shaped cells with one nucleus; it lines hollow organs such as the gut and blood vessels.
Why: P has striations, voluntary control and attachment to bones: skeletal. Q is striated, involuntary and has intercalated discs: cardiac. R has no striations and lines the gut and vessels: smooth.

Key point: Striated + voluntary = skeletal; striated + involuntary + intercalated discs = cardiac; non-striated = smooth.

format: experiment
sub_topic: Dependence of the action potential on sodium ions outside the axon
Q: An isolated nerve fibre is kept in a solution in which all the sodium ions outside the fibre are replaced by an ion that cannot pass through the membrane. The potassium concentrations are unchanged. A stimulus is then applied. What is the most likely result?
A) A larger action potential, because sodium normally blocks the impulse
B) An action potential of normal size, because the impulse depends only on potassium
C) Little or no action potential, because the rapid depolarisation depends on sodium ions rushing into the fibre, while the resting potential is almost unchanged
D) The resting potential becomes positive at once, because potassium ions rush into the fibre
KEY: C
SOL:
Answer: (C) Little or no action potential, because the rapid depolarisation depends on sodium ions rushing into the fibre, while the resting potential is almost unchanged.
Concept: At rest the axonal membrane is more permeable to K⁺ than to Na⁺, and the inside is negative (polarised). A stimulus opens Na⁺ channels; Na⁺ flows in and the inside becomes positive (depolarisation). This rise is the action potential. Then K⁺ flows out and the resting potential is restored.
Why: With no sodium outside, there is nothing to flow in when the Na⁺ channels open, so the membrane cannot depolarise and no action potential forms. The resting potential depends mainly on K⁺, whose concentrations are unchanged, so it stays nearly the same.
Why not the others: (A) Sodium entry is what makes the impulse; removing sodium cannot make it bigger. (B) The rising phase depends on Na⁺ entry; K⁺ only restores the resting state. (D) At rest K⁺ tends to leave the fibre, not enter it, and its concentrations have not changed.
Key point: Depolarisation = Na⁺ influx; repolarisation = K⁺ efflux.
