sub_topic: Conduction velocity of a motor nerve and the delay at the neuromuscular junction from two latencies
Q: In a nerve–muscle preparation, the motor nerve is stimulated at point P, 120 mm from the muscle, and the muscle starts to contract 5.0 ms later. When the nerve is stimulated at point Q, 30 mm from the muscle, the delay is 3.2 ms. What is the conduction velocity of the nerve, and how much of the delay is not due to conduction along the nerve (transmission at the junction and the start of contraction)?
A) 50 m s⁻¹; 3.2 ms
B) 50 m s⁻¹; 2.6 ms
C) 24 m s⁻¹; 0 ms
D) 25 m s⁻¹; 2.0 ms
KEY: B
SOL:
Answer: (B) 50 m s⁻¹; 2.6 ms.
Concept: A nerve impulse travels along an axon as a wave of depolarisation at a fixed speed. At the neuromuscular junction (a chemical synapse) the impulse is passed on by release and diffusion of acetylcholine, which takes extra time (synaptic delay). The total delay = conduction time + a fixed junction and muscle delay.
Given: distances 120 mm and 30 mm; delays 5.0 ms and 3.2 ms.
Formula/Principle: the fixed delay is the same for both points, so the difference in delays is the conduction time over the difference in distance; v = Δd/Δt.
Step 1: v = (120 − 30) mm ÷ (5.0 − 3.2) ms = 90 mm ÷ 1.8 ms = 50 mm ms⁻¹ = 50 m s⁻¹.
Step 2: Conduction time from Q = 30 mm ÷ 50 mm ms⁻¹ = 0.6 ms; fixed delay = 3.2 − 0.6 = 2.6 ms.
Why: Check with P: 120/50 = 2.4 ms + 2.6 ms = 5.0 ms. Using two points cancels the unknown fixed delay, which comes mainly from chemical transmission at the junction.
Why not the others: (A) It takes the whole delay from Q as the fixed delay, ignoring 0.6 ms of conduction. (C) 120 mm ÷ 5.0 ms treats all the delay as conduction. (D) The conduction velocity is wrong by a factor of two, so the fixed delay is also wrong.
Key point: Use two stimulation points: Δdistance ÷ Δlatency = conduction velocity; the rest is synaptic and muscle delay.

sub_topic: Haplodiploid sex determination in honey bees and how drones form sperm
Q: In honey bees, a fertilised egg develops into a female (queen or worker) and an unfertilised egg develops into a male (drone). Females have 32 chromosomes. Which statement is correct?
A) Drones have 32 chromosomes and form sperm by meiosis
B) A drone has a father and a grandmother but no mother
C) Drones are haploid with 16 chromosomes, form sperm by mitosis, and have a mother and grandparents but no father
D) Sex in honey bees is decided by X and Y chromosomes, as in humans
KEY: C
SOL:
Answer: (C) Drones are haploid with 16 chromosomes, form sperm by mitosis, and have a mother and grandparents but no father.
Concept: Honey bees show haplodiploid sex determination: the number of chromosome sets decides sex. Diploid individuals (2n = 32) are female; haploid individuals (n = 16), which develop by parthenogenesis from unfertilised eggs, are male.
Why: A drone develops from an unfertilised egg of the queen, so it has a mother but no father, while its mother had both parents, so it has grandparents. Being haploid, it cannot halve its chromosome number again, so it forms sperm by mitosis, and all its sperm are genetically identical.
Why not the others: (A) Drones are haploid (16 chromosomes); meiosis would halve this again. (B) The relationship is reversed: a drone has a mother, not a father. (D) Honey bees have no sex chromosomes; sex depends on ploidy.
Key point: Honey bee: fertilised egg → female (2n = 32); unfertilised egg → male (n = 16), sperm by mitosis.

sub_topic: Effect of gene flow on allele frequencies of two populations that had diverged by drift
Q: Two small, isolated populations of a rodent started with the same allele frequencies. After many generations, the frequency of allele A was 0.2 in one and 0.8 in the other, although their environments were similar. A corridor was then built so that animals could move between them, and after several generations the frequency of A was close to 0.5 in both. Which explanation is best?
A) Gene flow (migration) carried alleles between the populations and made their allele frequencies similar, undoing the divergence that genetic drift had caused
B) New mutations to allele A arose in one population and not in the other
C) Natural selection favoured the same allele in both populations after the corridor was built
D) A founder effect occurred when the corridor was built
KEY: A
SOL:
Answer: (A) Gene flow (migration) carried alleles between the populations and made their allele frequencies similar, undoing the divergence that genetic drift had caused.
Concept: Factors that change allele frequencies (Hardy–Weinberg disturbing factors) include gene flow, genetic drift, mutation, genetic recombination and natural selection. Drift, which is random change, is strong in small populations and makes isolated populations diverge. Gene flow mixes the gene pools and makes populations more alike.
Why: With similar environments, the original divergence is best explained by chance (drift) in small populations. Once migration begins, alleles move both ways, and the allele frequencies of the two populations converge towards a common value.
Why not the others: (B) Mutation is too slow to change allele frequencies by this much in a few generations. (C) Selection for the same allele would push both towards 1 or 0, not towards 0.5. (D) A founder effect needs a few individuals starting a new population; it would increase differences, not remove them.
Key point: Drift makes isolated populations diverge; gene flow makes them converge.

Q: One molecule of pyruvate enters the mitochondrion and is completely oxidised through the link reaction and the Krebs cycle, with the reduced coenzymes passing through the electron transport system. Taking 3 ATP per NADH and 2 ATP per FADH₂, how many ATP are formed in all, and how many molecules of O₂ are used?
A) 15 ATP; 2.5 O₂
B) 12 ATP; 2 O₂
C) 15 ATP; 5 O₂
D) 38 ATP; 6 O₂
KEY: A
SOL:
Answer: (A) 15 ATP; 2.5 O₂.
Concept: The link reaction (pyruvate → acetyl CoA) gives one NADH. The Krebs cycle gives 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂ and 1 GTP (ATP) per acetyl CoA. In the electron transport system, electrons from NADH and FADH₂ are passed to O₂, the final acceptor, and the energy is used for ATP synthesis (oxidative phosphorylation).
Given: per pyruvate: 4 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 GTP; 3 ATP per NADH, 2 ATP per FADH₂.
Formula/Principle: ATP = 3 × NADH + 2 × FADH₂ + substrate-level ATP; each NADH or FADH₂ gives 2 electrons, and ½ O₂ accepts 2 electrons.
Step 1: ATP = 3 × 4 + 2 × 1 + 1 = 15.
Step 2: Electron pairs = 4 + 1 = 5; O₂ = 5 × ½ = 2.5 molecules.
Why: Two pyruvates come from one glucose, so this gives 30 ATP and 5 O₂ from pyruvate; adding glycolysis gives the textbook 38 ATP and 6 O₂ per glucose.
Why not the others: (B) It counts only one acetyl CoA in the Krebs cycle and leaves out the NADH of the link reaction. (C) It takes one O₂ per electron pair instead of ½ O₂. (D) These are the values for a whole glucose molecule.
Key point: Per pyruvate: 4 NADH + 1 FADH₂ + 1 GTP → 15 ATP, using 2.5 O₂.
