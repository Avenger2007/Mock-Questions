B) Thorn of Bougainvillea and tendril of Cucurbita; eye of the octopus and eye of a mammal; sweet potato and potato
C) Forelimb of a whale and forelimb of a bat; flipper of a penguin and flipper of a dolphin; heart of a frog and heart of a bird
D) Thorn of Bougainvillea and tendril of Cucurbita; forelimb of a whale and forelimb of a bat; heart of a frog and heart of a bird
KEY: A
SOL:
Answer: (A) Eye of the octopus and eye of a mammal; flipper of a penguin and flipper of a dolphin; sweet potato and potato.
Concept: Analogous structures do the same job but have different origins; they result from convergent evolution. Homologous structures have the same origin but may do different jobs; they result from divergent evolution.
Why: The eyes of the octopus and the mammal, the flippers of the penguin and the dolphin, and the sweet potato (a modified root) and the potato (a modified stem) are all analogous: similar function, different origin. So every pair in A is convergent.
Why not the others: (B) The thorn of Bougainvillea and the tendril of Cucurbita are both modified stems (axillary buds), so they are homologous. (C) The forelimbs of a whale and a bat, and the hearts of vertebrates, are homologous. (D) All three pairs are homologous, which shows divergent evolution.
Key point: Same job, different origin = analogous (convergent); same origin = homologous (divergent).

Q: In a woodland, a sample of 1000 peppered moths taken before industrialisation had 900 light and 100 dark moths. After soot had darkened the tree trunks for many years, a sample of 1000 had 150 light and 850 dark moths. What type of natural selection does this show, and what is the percentage of dark moths in the later sample?
A) Stabilising selection; 85%
B) Disruptive selection; 15%
C) Directional selection; 85%
D) Directional selection; 15%
KEY: C
SOL:
Answer: (C) Directional selection; 85%.
Concept: Natural selection can be stabilising (the mean is favoured), directional (one extreme is favoured, so the mean shifts) or disruptive (both extremes are favoured). In industrial England, dark moths on dark trunks escaped predators, so their proportion rose.
Given: before, 900 light and 100 dark out of 1000; after, 150 light and 850 dark out of 1000.
Formula/Principle: percentage = (number of dark moths ÷ sample size) × 100.
Step 1: Dark moths rose from 10% to 85%, a shift towards one extreme.
Step 2: 850 ÷ 1000 × 100 = 85%.
Why: The population shifted from mostly light to mostly dark, which is a shift towards one extreme: directional selection. Dark moths in the later sample = 850 ÷ 1000 × 100 = 85%.
Why not the others: (A) Stabilising selection keeps the common type; here the common type was replaced. (B) Disruptive selection favours both extremes, and 15% is the share of light moths. (D) The type is right, but 15% is the share of light moths, not dark ones.
Key point: One extreme favoured, mean shifts = directional selection.

Q: In a malaria patient, assume that the cycle of Plasmodium in red blood cells takes 48 hours, that each infected cell bursts to release 16 merozoites, and that every merozoite infects a new red blood cell. A fever spike occurs each time infected cells burst. At the first spike, 1000 infected cells burst. How many red blood cells are infected just after the third spike, and how long after the first spike does the third spike occur?
A) \(2.6\times10^5\); 96 hours
B) \(4.1\times10^6\); 96 hours
C) \(4.1\times10^6\); 144 hours
D) \(4.8\times10^4\); 96 hours
KEY: B
SOL:
Answer: (B) \(4.1\times10^6\); 96 hours.
Concept: Sporozoites from the mosquito multiply first in liver cells and then in red blood cells. When infected red blood cells burst, they release merozoites and the toxic haemozoin, which causes the chill and high fever that recur in cycles.
Given: cycle = 48 h; 16 merozoites per burst cell; 1000 cells burst at the first spike.
Formula/Principle: newly infected cells after the k-th spike = 1000 × 16^k; time from spike 1 to spike k = (k − 1) × 48 h.
Step 1: After spike 3: 1000 × 16³ = 4 096 000 ≈ 4.1 × 10⁶.
Step 2: Time = (3 − 1) × 48 = 96 h.
Why: First spike: 1000 cells burst, giving 1000 × 16 = 16 000 newly infected cells. Second spike (48 h): 16 000 × 16 = 256 000 newly infected cells. Third spike (96 h): 256 000 × 16 = 4 096 000 ≈ \(4.1\times10^6\) newly infected cells. The third spike comes two cycles after the first: 2 × 48 = 96 hours.
Why not the others: (A) \(2.6\times10^5\) is the number just after the second spike. (C) The number is right, but three spikes span two intervals, not three. (D) It adds 16 000 three times instead of multiplying by 16 at each cycle.
Key point: Merozoite numbers multiply each cycle; n spikes span n − 1 cycles.
