B) CO (triple bond, bond order 3, about 113 pm) < CO₂ (bond order 2, about 115 pm) < CO₃²⁻ (bond order 1⅓, about 129 pm); the carbonate ion cannot be described by one Lewis structure — it is a resonance hybrid of three equivalent structures, so all three C–O bonds are equal in length, intermediate between single and double; the hybrid is lower in energy than any single canonical structure, the difference being the resonance energy, and canonical forms have no real existence
C) All three have equal C–O bond lengths because carbon is always tetravalent
D) In CO₃²⁻ one bond is double and two are single, so there are two different bond lengths
KEY: B
SOL:
Answer: (B) CO < CO₂ < CO₃²⁻; carbonate is a resonance hybrid with equal bonds of order 1⅓.
Concept: Bond order is given by the number of bonds between the two atoms in a molecule; with increase in bond order, bond enthalpy increases and bond length decreases; in CO there is a triple bond (bond order 3), in CO₂ each C–O bond is a double bond (bond order 2). It is often observed that a single Lewis structure is inadequate for the representation of a molecule in conformity with its experimentally determined parameters; according to the concept of resonance, whenever a single Lewis structure cannot describe a molecule accurately, a number of structures with similar energy, positions of nuclei, bonding and non-bonding pairs of electrons are taken as the canonical structures of the hybrid which describes the molecule accurately; the carbonate ion is a resonance hybrid of three canonical forms; resonance stabilises the molecule, as the energy of the resonance hybrid is less than the energy of any single canonical structure; resonance averages the bond characteristics as a whole; the energy difference between the actual structure and the lowest-energy resonance structure is called the resonance stabilisation energy or simply the resonance energy; the canonical forms have no real existence, and the molecule does not exist for a certain fraction of time in one canonical form and for other fractions of time in other canonical forms. For the carbonate ion, bond order = total bonds/number of bonding positions = 4/3 ≈ 1.33.
Why: Bond length shortens as more electron pairs are shared between the atoms, and resonance spreads the double-bond character equally over the three carbonate bonds.
Why not the others: (A) Order reversed. (C) Bond orders differ, so lengths differ. (D) Resonance makes all three bonds equal.
Key point: Higher bond order ⇒ shorter bond; resonance hybrids have averaged, equal bonds.
