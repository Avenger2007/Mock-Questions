sub_topic: Iodine needed to make the daily thyroxine output, and the disorder caused by iodine deficiency
Q: The thyroid gland of an adult secretes about 80 µg of thyroxine (T₄) per day. One molecule of thyroxine contains four iodine atoms, and its molar mass is 777 g mol⁻¹ (I = 127). Assuming that none of the iodine is recycled, what is the minimum daily intake of iodine needed to make this thyroxine, and which disorder appears when dietary iodine stays too low?
A) About 39 µg; goitre
B) About 52 µg; acromegaly
C) About 52 µg; goitre
D) About 80 µg; goitre
KEY: C
SOL:
Answer: (C) About 52 µg; goitre.
Concept: Thyroxine (tetraiodothyronine, T₄) and triiodothyronine (T₃) are iodine-containing hormones of the thyroid follicles. Iodine is essential for their synthesis. When dietary iodine is too low, little hormone is made, TSH from the pituitary stays high, and the thyroid enlarges (goitre).
Given: 80 µg T₄ per day; M(T₄) = 777 g mol⁻¹; 4 I atoms per molecule; I = 127.
Formula/Principle: mass of iodine = mass of T₄ × (4 × 127)/777.
Step 1: Mass fraction of iodine in T₄ = 508/777 = 0.654.
Step 2: Iodine needed = 80 µg × 0.654 ≈ 52 µg per day.
Why: About two-thirds of the mass of thyroxine is iodine, so 80 µg of hormone carries about 52 µg of iodine. Low iodine lowers hormone output, the negative feedback on TSH is lost, and continued TSH stimulation makes the gland grow.
Why not the others: (A) 39 µg uses three iodine atoms per molecule (that of T₃), not four. (B) Acromegaly comes from excess growth hormone in adults, not from iodine deficiency. (D) It treats the whole mass of thyroxine as iodine.
Key point: T₄ has 4 iodine atoms (about 65 % of its mass); iodine deficiency → low T₄, high TSH → goitre.

sub_topic: Ranking three populations by heterozygote frequency under Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium
Q: Three large, randomly mating populations are each in Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium for a gene with alleles A and a. The frequency of the recessive phenotype (aa) is 0.01 in population P, 0.25 in population Q and 0.64 in population R. Which is the correct order of the frequency of heterozygotes (Aa)?
A) R > Q > P
B) Q > R > P
C) P > R > Q
D) Q > P > R
KEY: B
SOL:
Answer: (B) Q > R > P.
Concept: In Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium the genotype frequencies are p² (AA), 2pq (Aa) and q² (aa), where p + q = 1. The heterozygote frequency 2pq is greatest (0.5) when p = q = 0.5.
Given: q² = 0.01 (P), 0.25 (Q), 0.64 (R).
Formula/Principle: q = √(q²); p = 1 − q; heterozygotes = 2pq.
Step 1: P: q = 0.1, p = 0.9, 2pq = 0.18.
Step 2: Q: q = 0.5, p = 0.5, 2pq = 0.50; R: q = 0.8, p = 0.2, 2pq = 0.32.
Why: The order is Q (0.50) > R (0.32) > P (0.18). Q has equal allele frequencies, which gives the largest 2pq. R and P are on opposite sides of 0.5, and R (q = 0.8) is closer to 0.5 than P (q = 0.1).
Why not the others: (A) This is the order of the recessive phenotype, not of heterozygotes. (C) It reverses the true order. (D) P has the most unequal allele frequencies, so it has the fewest heterozygotes.
Key point: 2pq is largest when p = q; the further q is from 0.5, the fewer heterozygotes.

Q: A PCR is run in which every cycle has the same efficiency. Samples are taken at three points, and the number of copies of the target DNA is measured.

10 | \(1.02\times10^{5}\)

SOL:
Answer: (C) About 100; 2 per cycle.

Step 1: 4.0/0.50 = 8 = 2³, so the mass doubles three times in 15 days: doubling time = 5 days.
Step 2: \(r=\ln2/5=0.693/5\approx0.14\) per day; 20 days = 4 doublings, so \(W_{20}=0.50\times2^{4}=8.0\) g.
Why: In geometric growth the mass increases by the same factor in equal times, so the 15-day factor of 8 means a factor of 2 every 5 days. Later, shoots or roots formed from the callus would be redifferentiation.
Why not the others: (A) It assumes arithmetic (linear) growth of 3.5 g in 15 days. (B) 16 g corresponds to five doublings, i.e. 25 days. (D) 0.70 is ln 2, not ln 2 divided by the doubling time.
Key point: Exponential growth: equal factors in equal times; \(r=\ln2/\text{doubling time}\).

sub_topic: Ranking lung capacities from the four primary lung volumes
Q: For a healthy adult, tidal volume = 500 mL, inspiratory reserve volume = 2500 mL, expiratory reserve volume = 1000 mL and residual volume = 1200 mL. Consider these capacities: P inspiratory capacity; Q functional residual capacity; R expiratory capacity; S vital capacity. Which is the correct increasing order?
A) R < Q < P < S
B) Q < R < P < S
C) R < P < Q < S
D) R < Q < S < P
KEY: A
SOL:
Answer: (A) R < Q < P < S.
Concept: Lung capacities are sums of primary volumes: inspiratory capacity IC = TV + IRV; expiratory capacity EC = TV + ERV; functional residual capacity FRC = ERV + RV; vital capacity VC = ERV + TV + IRV.
Given: TV = 500, IRV = 2500, ERV = 1000, RV = 1200 (mL).
Formula/Principle: add the volumes that make up each capacity.
Step 1: R (EC) = 500 + 1000 = 1500 mL; Q (FRC) = 1000 + 1200 = 2200 mL.
Step 2: P (IC) = 500 + 2500 = 3000 mL; S (VC) = 1000 + 500 + 2500 = 4000 mL.
Why: The order is 1500 < 2200 < 3000 < 4000, i.e. R < Q < P < S. Vital capacity includes every volume except the residual volume, so it is the largest of these four.
Why not the others: (B) FRC (2200) is larger than EC (1500). (C) IC (3000) is larger than FRC (2200). (D) VC contains IC plus ERV, so VC must exceed IC.
Key point: IC = TV + IRV; EC = TV + ERV; FRC = ERV + RV; VC = TV + IRV + ERV.

@@ Q19
format: conceptual
sub_topic: State of the heart valves during ventricular systole and the pressure differences that cause it
Q: During ventricular systole (after the initial short phase in which all valves are shut), which description of the heart valves and its cause is correct?
A) The atrioventricular valves are open because atrial pressure exceeds ventricular pressure, and the semilunar valves are closed
B) The tricuspid and bicuspid valves are closed because ventricular pressure exceeds atrial pressure, and the semilunar valves are open because ventricular pressure exceeds the pressure in the pulmonary artery and aorta
C) All four valves stay closed during the whole of systole
D) All four valves are open so that blood can move freely through the heart
KEY: B
SOL:
Answer: (B) The tricuspid and bicuspid valves are closed because ventricular pressure exceeds atrial pressure, and the semilunar valves are open because ventricular pressure exceeds the pressure in the pulmonary artery and aorta.
Concept: Heart valves open and close passively according to the pressure on their two sides, and they allow flow in one direction only. This keeps the double circulation one-way: right ventricle → lungs, left ventricle → body.
Why: When the ventricles contract, ventricular pressure rises above atrial pressure and the atrioventricular valves close (first heart sound, "lub"). When ventricular pressure exceeds the pressure in the pulmonary artery and aorta, the semilunar valves open and blood is ejected. At the end of systole the semilunar valves close ("dub").
Why not the others: (A) This describes ventricular diastole, when the ventricles fill. (C) All valves are shut only for a short time at the start of systole; blood could not be ejected if they stayed shut. (D) Open atrioventricular valves during systole would let blood flow back into the atria.
Key point: Systole: AV valves closed, semilunar valves open; diastole: the reverse.

@@ Q20
format: data
sub_topic: Interpreting the composition of plasma, glomerular filtrate and urine
Q: The table gives the concentration (g per 100 mL) of some substances in the blood plasma, the glomerular filtrate and the urine of a healthy person.

Substance | Plasma | Glomerular filtrate | Urine
Protein | 8.0 | 0 | 0
Glucose | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0
Urea | 0.03 | 0.03 | 2.0
Sodium ions | 0.30 | 0.30 | 0.35

Which conclusion is correct?
A) Glucose is not filtered at the glomerulus
B) Proteins are filtered and then completely reabsorbed in the tubule
C) Urea is not filtered; it enters the urine only by secretion
D) Proteins are not filtered; glucose is filtered and completely reabsorbed; urea becomes about 67 times more concentrated, mainly because most of the filtered water is reabsorbed
KEY: D
SOL:
Answer: (D) Proteins are not filtered; glucose is filtered and completely reabsorbed; urea becomes about 67 times more concentrated, mainly because most of the filtered water is reabsorbed.
Concept: Glomerular filtration is ultrafiltration: blood cells and plasma proteins stay in the blood, while small solutes pass into the filtrate at the same concentration as in plasma. The tubule then reabsorbs useful substances (glucose, amino acids, much Na⁺ and about 99 % of the water).
Why: Protein is 8.0 in plasma but 0 in filtrate, so it is not filtered. Glucose is 0.10 in filtrate but 0 in urine, so it is entirely reabsorbed (in the proximal convoluted tubule). Urea is at the same concentration in plasma and filtrate, so it is freely filtered; its concentration in urine is 2.0/0.03 ≈ 67 times higher because water is removed from the tubular fluid.
Why not the others: (A) Glucose has the same concentration in filtrate as in plasma, so it is filtered. (B) Protein is already 0 in the filtrate, so it never enters the tubule. (C) Urea is present in the filtrate at the plasma concentration, so it is filtered.
Key point: Filtrate ≈ protein-free plasma; glucose fully reabsorbed; urea concentrated by water reabsorption.
