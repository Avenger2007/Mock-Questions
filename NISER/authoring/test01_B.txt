sub_topic: Three domains of life: what places an organism in a domain
Q: A newly isolated unicellular organism has no nuclear membrane. Its cell wall has no peptidoglycan, and the sequence of its small-subunit ribosomal RNA is closer to that of yeast than to that of Escherichia coli. In the three-domain system, where is it placed, and why?
A) Bacteria, because every cell without a nuclear membrane belongs to this domain
B) Archaea, because the domains are separated by ribosomal RNA sequence and cell chemistry, not only by the absence of a nucleus
C) Eukarya, because its ribosomal RNA is closer to that of a eukaryote than to that of a bacterium
D) Kingdom Monera of the domain Eukarya, because Monera contains all organisms without a nucleus
KEY: B
SOL:
Answer: (B) Archaea, because the domains are separated by ribosomal RNA sequence and cell chemistry, not only by the absence of a nucleus.
Concept: The three-domain system divides life into Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya. Both Bacteria and Archaea are prokaryotic, so the absence of a nucleus cannot separate them. They are separated by ribosomal RNA sequence and by cell-wall and membrane chemistry.
Why: The organism is prokaryotic, so it is not in Eukarya. Its wall lacks peptidoglycan, which is the wall polymer of Bacteria, and its ribosomal RNA is nearer to a eukaryote than to a bacterium. Both observations are features of Archaea. The five-kingdom system would put it in Monera, but the three-domain system splits Monera into two domains.
Why not the others: (A) A missing nucleus is shared by Bacteria and Archaea; the wall without peptidoglycan rules out Bacteria. (C) Being closer to eukaryotes in one sequence does not make a cell eukaryotic; it still has no nucleus. (D) Monera is a kingdom of the five-kingdom system and is not a part of the domain Eukarya.
Key point: Prokaryote is a cell type, not a domain; Archaea and Bacteria are told apart by ribosomal RNA and wall chemistry.

(D) The retentate does contain lipids; their presence is explained by membrane vesicles, not by a wrong identification.
Key point: Lipids are in the macromolecular fraction because of membrane vesicles, not because they are polymers.

Q: Cells of a diploid organism have 2n = 8, and a cell in the G1 phase has 4 pg of DNA. A dividing culture is treated with a drug that prevents formation of spindle fibres but does not affect any other event. After some hours most cells are found stopped at one stage. A) 4 pg, 8 chromosomes, 8 chromatids
B) 8 pg, 16 chromosomes, 16 chromatids
C) 4 pg, 4 chromosomes, 8 chromatids
D) 8 pg, 8 chromosomes, 16 chromatids
KEY: D
SOL:
Answer: (D) 8 pg, 8 chromosomes, 16 chromatids.
Concept: DNA is replicated in the S phase, so the DNA content doubles from 2C to 4C, but the number of chromosomes does not change because the two sister chromatids stay joined at the centromere. Chromatids separate only in anaphase, and this needs spindle fibres.

Key point: S phase doubles DNA and chromatids, not chromosome number; anaphase doubles chromosome number.

sub_topic: Segregation with a lethal genotype: a 2 : 1 ratio
Q: In mice, crosses between two yellow mice always give yellow and grey young in the ratio 2 : 1. Crosses between yellow and grey mice always give yellow and grey young in the ratio 1 : 1. Grey × grey gives only grey. Coat colour is controlled by one gene with two alleles. If yellow × yellow crosses produce 120 live young in total, what is expected?
A) 90 yellow and 30 grey; one-third of the yellow young are homozygous
B) 60 yellow and 60 grey; the two alleles are co-dominant
C) 80 yellow and 40 grey; half of the yellow young are homozygous
D) 80 yellow and 40 grey; all the yellow young are heterozygous, because embryos homozygous for the yellow allele do not survive
KEY: D
SOL:
Answer: (D) 80 yellow and 40 grey; all the yellow young are heterozygous, because embryos homozygous for the yellow allele do not survive.
Concept: By the law of segregation, a cross of two heterozygotes (Yy × Yy) gives the genotypes YY : Yy : yy in the ratio 1 : 2 : 1. If one genotype does not survive, the ratio among the live young changes.
Why: Grey × grey gives only grey, so grey is the recessive homozygote yy. Yellow × grey always gives 1 : 1, so every yellow mouse tested is Yy; no true-breeding yellow mouse exists. Yy × Yy gives 1 YY : 2 Yy : 1 yy. The observed 2 yellow : 1 grey is obtained if the YY class is missing. Of 120 live young, yellow = 2/3 × 120 = 80, all Yy, and grey = 1/3 × 120 = 40.
Why not the others: (A) 90 : 30 is the 3 : 1 ratio, which is not observed; a homozygous yellow would give only yellow young with grey, which never happens. (B) Co-dominance would give a third phenotype, and 1 : 1 is the result of yellow × grey, not yellow × yellow. (C) The numbers are right, but a homozygous yellow mouse would not give the 1 : 1 ratio seen in every yellow × grey cross.
Key point: A 2 : 1 ratio from a cross of two heterozygotes means one homozygous class is lethal.

Why: Both the plasmid and the donor DNA must be cut with the same enzyme so that their ends are complementary. On mixing, the ends of the gene pair with the ends of the opened plasmid. Ligase seals the nicks and gives a stable recombinant plasmid, which is then introduced into the host.
Why not the others: (B) Ligase has no free ends to join in uncut DNA, and cutting afterwards only opens the molecules again. (C) An uncut circular plasmid has no free sticky ends, so the gene cannot be inserted into it. (D) Ligase added to the medium does not join the pieces before they enter the cells; the joining must be done before transformation.
Key point: Same enzyme for vector and insert → complementary sticky ends → ligase → transformation.

Q: A pond had 400 fish at the start of a year. During the year there were 160 births and 80 deaths; 20 fish entered from a connected stream and 40 fish left through it. What are the per capita birth rate, the per capita death rate and the number of fish at the end of the year?
A) 0.4 per fish per year; 0.2 per fish per year; 480
B) 160 per fish per year; 80 per fish per year; 460
C) 0.4 per fish per year; 0.2 per fish per year; 460
D) 0.35 per fish per year; 0.17 per fish per year; 460
KEY: C
SOL:
Answer: (C) 0.4 per fish per year; 0.2 per fish per year; 460.
Concept: Birth rate and death rate of a population are expressed per individual (per capita). Population density changes by natality and immigration, which add individuals, and mortality and emigration, which remove them.
Given: \(N_0=400,\ B=160,\ D=80,\ I=20,\ E=40\) in one year.
Formula/Principle: \(b=\dfrac{B}{N_0},\quad d=\dfrac{D}{N_0},\quad N_1=N_0+(B+I)-(D+E)\)
Step 1: \(b=\dfrac{160}{400}=0.4\) per fish per year.
Step 2: \(d=\dfrac{80}{400}=0.2\) per fish per year.
Step 3: \[N_1=400+(160+20)-(80+40)=400+180-120=460\]
Why: The rates are found by dividing by the population at the start, and all four processes are counted in the final number.
Why not the others: (A) 480 = 400 + 160 − 80 leaves out immigration and emigration. (B) 160 and 80 are total births and deaths, not per capita rates. (D) 0.35 and 0.17 are obtained by dividing by the final number 460 instead of the starting number 400.
Key point: N(t+1) = N(t) + [(B + I) − (D + E)].

@@ Q20
format: numerical
sub_topic: Species–area relationship: effect of the slope Z
Q: The species–area relationship is log S = log C + Z log A. A survey of patches inside one region gave Z = 0.15. A survey of very large areas, such as whole continents, gave Z = 1.0. If the area sampled is increased 100 times in each survey, by what factor does species richness increase? (10^0.3 ≈ 2)
A) About 15 times in the first survey and 100 times in the second
B) About 2 times in both surveys
C) About 100 times in the first survey and 2 times in the second
D) About 2 times in the first survey and 100 times in the second
KEY: D
SOL:
Answer: (D) About 2 times in the first survey and 100 times in the second.
Concept: Alexander von Humboldt observed that species richness increases with explored area, but only up to a limit. The relation is S = C A^Z, a straight line on a log scale with slope Z. Z is 0.1 to 0.2 for small areas inside a region and 0.6 to 1.2 for very large areas such as whole continents.
Given: \(S=CA^{Z};\ \dfrac{A_2}{A_1}=100;\ Z_1=0.15,\ Z_2=1.0\)
Formula/Principle: \(\dfrac{S_2}{S_1}=\left(\dfrac{A_2}{A_1}\right)^{Z}\)
Step 1: \(\left(100\right)^{0.15}=\left(10^{2}\right)^{0.15}=10^{0.3}\approx 2\)
Step 2: \[\left(100\right)^{1.0}=100\]
Why: The same increase in area gives a much larger increase in species when the slope Z is steeper.
Why not the others: (A) 15 comes from multiplying 100 by 0.15; Z is an exponent, not a multiplier. (B) The second survey has Z = 1.0, which gives 100 times, not 2 times. (C) The two surveys are interchanged.
Key point: S = C A^Z; Z is about 0.1 to 0.2 within a region and 0.6 to 1.2 across continents.
