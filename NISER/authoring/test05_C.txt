Q: For the complex \(\ce{K3[Fe(C2O4)3]}\), what are the oxidation state of iron, the coordination number of iron, and the number of ions formed per formula unit in aqueous solution?

B) +3; 6; 4
C) +6; 6; 2
D) +2; 6; 4
KEY: B
SOL:
Answer: (B) +3; 6; 4.
Concept: In Werner's theory, the primary valence of the metal (its oxidation state) is satisfied by negative ions, and the secondary valence (coordination number) is the number of donor atoms bound to the metal. A bidentate ligand such as oxalate, \(\ce{C2O4^2-}\), binds through two donor atoms.
Why: The complex ion is [Fe(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻ (balanced by 3 K⁺). Oxidation state: x + 3(−2) = −3, so x = +3. Three bidentate oxalates give 3 × 2 = 6 donor atoms, so the coordination number is 6. In water it gives 3 K⁺ + 1 [Fe(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻ = 4 ions.
Why not the others: (A) It counts ligands instead of donor atoms; each oxalate binds through two oxygen atoms. (C) Fe is +3, and the salt gives 4 ions, not 2. (D) Three oxalates (−6) and a −3 complex ion need Fe³⁺, not Fe²⁺.
Key point: Coordination number = number of donor atoms (oxalate counts 2), not number of ligands.

Q: Three amines X, Y and Z are ethanamine, N-methylethanamine and aniline, in some order. Each is treated with NaNO₂ and dilute HCl at 0–5 °C.

Y | A yellow oily layer separates

A) X ethanamine, Y N-methylethanamine, Z aniline
B) X aniline, Y ethanamine, Z N-methylethanamine

D) X ethanamine, Y aniline, Z N-methylethanamine

SOL:
Answer: (A) X ethanamine, Y N-methylethanamine, Z aniline.
