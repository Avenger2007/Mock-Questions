sub_topic: Converting the composition of a glucose solution of known density into mass percentage, molality, molarity and mole fraction, and noting which terms depend on temperature
Q: 18.0 g of glucose (M = 180) is dissolved in 100 g of water; the solution has density 1.10 g mL⁻¹. Which set of values is correct?
A) Mass % = 18.0; molality = 0.100 m; molarity = 1.00 M; mole fraction of glucose = 0.10
B) Mass % = 15.3; molality = 1.00 mol kg⁻¹; molarity = 0.932 mol L⁻¹ (solution volume 118 g ÷ 1.10 g mL⁻¹ = 107.3 mL); mole fraction of glucose = 0.100/(0.100 + 5.56) = 0.0177; molarity changes with temperature because volume does, while molality and mole fraction do not
C) Mass % = 15.3; molality = 0.932 m; molarity = 1.00 M; mole fraction = 0.018
D) Mass % = 18.0; molality = 1.00 m; molarity = 1.00 M, since 1 kg of water is 1 L of solution
KEY: B
SOL:
Answer: (B) 15.3 %; 1.00 m; 0.932 M; x = 0.0177.
Concept: Mass percentage of a component = mass of the component in the solution ÷ total mass of the solution × 100. Mole fraction of a component = number of moles of the component ÷ total number of moles of all the components. Molarity (M) is the number of moles of solute dissolved in one litre of solution; molality (m) is the number of moles of the solute per kilogram of the solvent. Mass %, ppm, mole fraction and molality are independent of temperature, whereas molarity is a function of temperature, because volume depends on temperature and mass does not.
Given: m(glucose) = 18.0 g ⇒ 0.100 mol; m(water) = 100 g ⇒ 5.56 mol; density 1.10 g mL⁻¹.
Formula/Principle: Mass % = 100 × m_solute/m_solution; m = n/kg solvent; M = n/V(L); x = n/n_total.
Step 1: Mass of solution = 118 g ⇒ mass % = 18.0/118 × 100 = 15.3 %.
Step 2: Molality = 0.100/0.100 kg = 1.00 mol kg⁻¹; volume = 118/1.10 = 107.3 mL ⇒ molarity = 0.100/0.1073 = 0.932 mol L⁻¹.
Step 3: x(glucose) = 0.100/5.66 = 0.0177.
Why: Molality uses the solvent mass and molarity the solution volume, which includes the solute and depends on density.
Why not the others: (A) Uses solvent mass for mass % and mixes up molality and molarity. (C) Molality and molarity swapped. (D) Solution volume is not 1 L per kg of water.
Key point: Molality per kg solvent; molarity per L solution (needs density); only molarity varies with temperature.
