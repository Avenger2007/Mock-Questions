H₂SO₅ is HO–O–SO₂–OH: taking all O as −2 gives +8, which is impossible for S (only 6 valence electrons); with two O at −1 (peroxide), 2(+1) + x + 3(−2) + 2(−1) = 0 gives x = +6.
Why not the others: (A) Atoms cannot really be in a fractional state; +2.5 is only an average of +5 and 0. (B) S cannot exceed +6, its number of valence electrons; the peroxide O atoms are −1. (D) If all were +5, the ion's charge would be +8, not −2.
Key point: Fractional oxidation numbers are averages; peroxide oxygen is −1, so S in H₂SO₅ is +6.

KEY: D
SOL:
Answer: (D) 100.26 °C; 14.6 g.
Concept: Depression of freezing point and elevation of boiling point are colligative properties: they depend on the total molality of solute particles. For an electrolyte, ΔT = i K m, where i is the van 't Hoff factor (i = 2 for fully dissociated NaCl).

Why not the others: (A) The boiling point is right, but taking i = 1 doubles the mass of NaCl. (B) Uses a particle molality of 1.0 for the boiling point, twice the true value. (C) Takes ΔTb equal to ΔTf, ignoring the different constants.
Key point: ΔTb/ΔTf = Kb/Kf for the same solution; divide the particle molality by i to get the salt molality.

Q: In the laboratory, uranium compounds can be prepared with uranium in the +3, +4, +5 and +6 states (for example, yellow UO₂²⁺ salts), whereas compounds of most lanthanides, such as neodymium, are almost always in the +3 state. Which explanation is correct?
