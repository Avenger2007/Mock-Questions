sub_topic: Living and dead elements of xylem
question: Xylem is made up of tracheids, vessels, xylem fibres and xylem parenchyma. Which of these is living at maturity, and which is absent from the xylem of gymnosperms?
A) Tracheids are living; fibres are absent in gymnosperms
B) Vessels are living; tracheids are absent in gymnosperms
C) Xylem parenchyma is living; vessels are absent in gymnosperms
D) Xylem fibres are living; parenchyma is absent in gymnosperms
key: C

@@ Q15
Answer: (C) Xylem parenchyma is living; vessels are absent in gymnosperms.
Why: Tracheids, vessels and xylem fibres have thick lignified walls and lose their protoplasm, so they are dead when they function. Xylem parenchyma cells are living, thin-walled cells that store food as starch or fat and help the sideways movement of water. Vessels, long tubes made of many cells joined end to end, are a feature of angiosperms; gymnosperms conduct water through tracheids only.
Why not the others: (A) Tracheids are dead at maturity, and gymnosperms do have fibres. (B) Vessels are dead at maturity, and tracheids are the main water-conducting cells of gymnosperms. (D) Xylem fibres are dead cells, and gymnosperms do have xylem parenchyma.
Key point: Only xylem parenchyma is living; vessels are characteristic of angiosperms.
