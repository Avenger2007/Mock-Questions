@@Q Q54
sub_topic: Kirchhoff's junction rule and conservation of charge
question: Four wires meet at a junction in a steady-current circuit. Currents of 2.0 A and 3.0 A flow into the junction through two of the wires, and 4.0 A flows out through the third. What is the current in the fourth wire, and which conservation law does the rule used express?
A) 1.0 A into the junction; conservation of energy
B) 1.0 A out of the junction; conservation of charge
C) 9.0 A out of the junction; conservation of charge
D) 1.0 A out of the junction; conservation of energy
key: B

@@ Q54
Answer: (B) 1.0 A out of the junction; conservation of charge.
Given: \(I_{\text{in}}=2.0\ \mathrm A,\ 3.0\ \mathrm A;\quad I_{\text{out}}=4.0\ \mathrm A;\quad\text{steady currents}\)
Formula/Principle: \(\displaystyle\sum I_{\text{in}}=\sum I_{\text{out}}\)
Step 1: \(2.0+3.0=4.0+I_4\)
Worked derivation: \[I_4=5.0-4.0=1.0\ \mathrm A\ \ (\text{out of the junction})\]
Check: \[\text{in}:\ 5.0\ \mathrm A;\quad\text{out}:\ 4.0+1.0=5.0\ \mathrm A\]
Why: \(\text{no charge accumulates at a junction in a steady state}\Rightarrow\dfrac{dq}{dt}=0\)
Why not the others: (A) \(I_4\ \text{into the junction}:\ 6.0\ \mathrm A\ne4.0\ \mathrm A\); (C) \(9.0=2.0+3.0+4.0\) (all currents added); (D) \(\text{energy conservation gives the loop rule},\ \sum\Delta V=0\).
Key point: \(\text{junction rule}:\ \text{charge};\quad\text{loop rule}:\ \text{energy}\)
