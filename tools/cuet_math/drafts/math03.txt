=

@@ AM8|Formulating linear programming problems|m|s|D|lpp formulation statements
Consider the following statements:
(A) In a diet problem, the objective is usually to minimise cost.
(B) Non-negativity restrictions are used because the quantities cannot be negative.
(C) In a transportation problem, the objective is usually to minimise the total transport cost.
(D) The constraints of a linear programming problem may contain terms such as \(x^2\).
Which of the statements given above are correct?
A) (A) and (B) only
B) (B), (C) and (D) only
C) (A), (B), (C) and (D)
D) (A), (B) and (C) only
=
Why: (A), (B) and (C) describe standard LPP formulations correctly.
Why not the others: (D) is false: every constraint of an LPP must be linear. Options (B) and (C) include (D); (A) leaves out (C).
Key point: "Linear" applies to the objective function and to every constraint.

@@ AM8|Maximum profit from chairs and tables|h|n|C|lpp product mix
A workshop makes chairs (profit ₹30 each) and tables (profit ₹50 each). A chair takes 1 hour and a table 2 hours, and 20 hours are available. At most 10 chairs can be sold. The maximum profit is:
A) ₹500
B) ₹300
C) ₹550
D) ₹600
=
Given: maximise \(Z = 30x + 50y\) subject to \(x + 2y \le 20\), \(x \le 10\), \(x, y \ge 0\)
Formula: evaluate \(Z\) at the corner points
Step 1: Corners: \((0, 0)\), \((10, 0)\), \((10, 5)\), \((0, 10)\)
Step 2: \(Z = 0, 300, 550, 500\)
Step 3: Maximum ₹550 at 10 chairs and 5 tables
Common mistake: ₹500 (A) makes only tables. ₹600 (D) makes 20 chairs, ignoring \(x \le 10\).
Key point: Include every limit (here the sales limit) as a constraint.
