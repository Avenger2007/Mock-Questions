@@ C1|Sum of a matrix and its transpose|e|n|B|a plus transpose symmetric
If \(A = \begin{bmatrix}2&-1\\3&4\end{bmatrix}\), then \(A + A^T\) is:

B) \(\begin{bmatrix}4&2\\2&8\end{bmatrix}\)
C) \(\begin{bmatrix}0&-4\\4&0\end{bmatrix}\)

=

@@ C1|Expanding a 3 × 3 determinant|m|n|A|det 3x3 expand

=

@@ C1|Inverse from a matrix equation|h|n|D|inverse from matrix equation

@@ C2|Monotonicity of standard functions|m|s|D|monotonic standard functions
Consider the following statements:

(B) \(\log x\) is strictly increasing on \((0, \infty)\).
(C) \(\dfrac{1}{x}\) is strictly decreasing on \((0, \infty)\).
(D) \(x^2\) is strictly increasing on \(\mathbb{R}\).
Which of the statements given above is/are NOT correct?
A) (C) only
B) (C) and (D) only
C) (B) and (D) only
D) (D) only
=
Why: Only (D) is false: \(\dfrac{d}{dx}x^2 = 2x < 0\) for \(x < 0\), so \(x^2\) decreases on \((-\infty, 0)\).

Key point: A function is increasing on an interval only if its derivative is positive throughout that interval.

@@ C4|Checking proposed solutions of differential equations|h|s|C|verify de solutions

(A) \(y = e^{2x}\) is a solution of \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2y\).
(B) \(y = \sin x\) is a solution of \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} + y = 0\).
(C) \(y = x^2\) is a solution of \(x\dfrac{dy}{dx} = y\).
(D) The general solution of a second-order differential equation contains two arbitrary constants.

B) (B), (C) and (D) only
C) (A), (B) and (D) only
D) (A), (B), (C) and (D)

@@ M1|A relation defined by an even difference|e|d|B|parity equivalence
On the set of integers \(\mathbb{Z}\), the relation \(R = \{(a, b) : a - b \text{ is even}\}\) is:
A) Reflexive and symmetric but not transitive
B) An equivalence relation
C) Symmetric but not reflexive
D) Transitive but not symmetric
=
Why: \(a - a = 0\) is even (reflexive). If \(a - b\) is even, so is \(b - a\) (symmetric). If \(a - b\) and \(b - c\) are even, their sum \(a - c\) is even (transitive).

Key point: "Same remainder on division by \(n\)" relations are always equivalence relations.

B) ₹966.20
C) ₹1,020.00
D) ₹1,035.67
=
Given: coupon ₹100 per year, redemption ₹1,000 after 2 years, \(i = 0.08\)
Formula: value \(= \dfrac{C}{1 + i} + \dfrac{C + F}{(1 + i)^2}\)
Step 1: \(\dfrac{100}{1.08} \approx 92.59\)
Step 2: \(\dfrac{1100}{1.1664} \approx 943.07\)
Step 3: Value \(\approx 92.59 + 943.07 = ₹1{,}035.67\)
Common mistake: ₹1,000 (A) is the par value, correct only when the yield equals the coupon rate. ₹966.20 (B) is the value at a 12% yield.
Key point: A bond whose coupon rate exceeds the market yield sells at a premium.

@@ AM8|Vocabulary of linear programming|m|m|C|lpp terms match
Match List-I with List-II.

List-I
(A) Decision variables
(B) Constraints
(C) Non-negativity restrictions
(D) Unbounded feasible region

List-II
(I) Linear inequalities that limit the variables
(II) The quantities whose values are to be decided
(III) A region that cannot be enclosed in a circle
(IV) The conditions \(x \ge 0\), \(y \ge 0\)
A) (A)-(I), (B)-(II), (C)-(IV), (D)-(III)
B) (A)-(II), (B)-(IV), (C)-(I), (D)-(III)
C) (A)-(II), (B)-(I), (C)-(IV), (D)-(III)
D) (A)-(II), (B)-(I), (C)-(III), (D)-(IV)
=
Why: Decision variables are the unknown quantities (II); constraints are the limiting inequalities (I); non-negativity means \(x, y \ge 0\) (IV); an unbounded region cannot be enclosed in any circle (III).
Why not the others: (A) swaps variables and constraints. (B) swaps constraints and non-negativity. (D) swaps the last two.
Key point: Non-negativity is a constraint too, but it gets its own name.

@@ AM8|Minimum cost of a diet|h|n|B|diet problem min cost
Food P contains 3 units of vitamin A and 2 units of vitamin B per kg; food Q contains 4 units of A and 1 unit of B per kg. A diet needs at least 18 units of A and 8 units of B. P costs ₹40 per kg and Q costs ₹30 per kg. The minimum cost of the diet is:
A) ₹240
B) ₹184
C) ₹168
D) ₹200
=
Given: minimise \(Z = 40x + 30y\) subject to \(3x + 4y \ge 18\), \(2x + y \ge 8\), \(x, y \ge 0\)
Formula: evaluate \(Z\) at the corners of the (unbounded) feasible region
Step 1: Corners: \((0, 8)\), the intersection of the two lines, and \((6, 0)\)
Step 2: Intersection: \(y = 8 - 2x\) in \(3x + 4y = 18\): \(3x + 32 - 8x = 18 \Rightarrow x = 2.8\), \(y = 2.4\)
Step 3: \(Z(0, 8) = 240\); \(Z(2.8, 2.4) = 112 + 72 = 184\); \(Z(6, 0) = 240\)
Step 4: The half-plane \(40x + 30y < 184\) has no feasible point, so the minimum is ₹184
Common mistake: ₹240 (A) checks only the corners on the axes.
Key point: For an unbounded region, finish with the half-plane check.
