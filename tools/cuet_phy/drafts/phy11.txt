@@ P1|What a dielectric does to an isolated charged capacitor|m|m|C|dielectric isolated capacitor effects match
A charged parallel plate capacitor (capacitance \(C\), voltage \(V\), energy \(U\)) is disconnected from its battery, and its gap is then filled with a dielectric of constant \(K\). Match each quantity in List-I with its new value in List-II.

List-I
(A) Capacitance
(B) Charge
(C) Potential difference
(D) Stored energy

List-II
(I) unchanged
(II) \(\dfrac{U}{K}\)
(III) \(KC\)
(IV) \(\dfrac{V}{K}\)
A) (A)-(III), (B)-(I), (C)-(II), (D)-(IV)
B) (A)-(I), (B)-(III), (C)-(IV), (D)-(II)
C) (A)-(III), (B)-(I), (C)-(IV), (D)-(II)

=
Step 1: The dielectric multiplies the capacitance: \(KC\) (III)

@@ P1|Potential energy, work and potential — which hold|m|s|B|zero field not zero potential,like charges separate pe falls
Consider the following statements:
(A) The work done by an electrostatic force between two points does not depend on the path.
(B) The potential energy of two like charges decreases as they are moved apart.
(C) Potentials due to several charges add algebraically.
(D) The electric potential is zero wherever the electric field is zero.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
A) (A) and (C) only
B) (A), (B) and (C) only
C) (B), (C) and (D) only
D) (A), (B), (C) and (D)
=
Why: (A) the electrostatic force is conservative. (B) \(U = \dfrac{kq_1q_2}{r}\) is positive and falls as \(r\) grows. (C) potential is a scalar.
Why not the others: (D) is false: inside a charged shell \(E = 0\) but \(V = \dfrac{kQ}{R} \ne 0\). Options (C) and (D) include (D); (A) leaves out (B).
Key point: \(E\) is the slope of \(V\); a zero slope does not mean a zero value.

@@ P1|Capacitance with two dielectric layers stacked between the plates|h|n|C|stacked dielectric layers capacitance
A parallel plate capacitor has a capacitance of 6 pF in air. Its gap is filled with two slabs, each half the gap thick, of dielectric constants 2 and 4, one above the other. The new capacitance is:
A) 18 pF
B) 36 pF
C) 16 pF
D) 24 pF
=
Given: \(C_0 = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0A}{d} = 6\) pF; layers of thickness \(\dfrac{d}{2}\), \(K_1 = 2\), \(K_2 = 4\)
Formula: stacked layers act in series: \(C = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0A}{\frac{d}{2K_1} + \frac{d}{2K_2}}\)
Step 1: \(\dfrac{1}{2 \times 2} + \dfrac{1}{2 \times 4} = \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{8} = \dfrac{3}{8}\)

Common mistake: 18 pF (A) averages \(K\) as for slabs side by side (in parallel); stacked slabs are in series.
Key point: Layers across the gap are in series; strips along the plates are in parallel.

@@ P2|Best grouping of 12 cells for the largest current|h|n|C|best grouping cells maximum current
