@@ P1|Third charge that holds two like charges in equilibrium|h|n|D|three charge equilibrium
Charges \(+q\) and \(+9q\) are fixed 16 cm apart. A third charge \(Q\) is placed on the line between them so that all three charges are in equilibrium. The value of \(Q\) is:
A) \(+\dfrac{9q}{16}\)
B) \(-\dfrac{3q}{4}\)
C) \(-\dfrac{q}{16}\)
D) \(-\dfrac{9q}{16}\)
=
Given: \(Q\) at \(x\) from \(+q\), so \(16 - x\) from \(+9q\)
Formula: equilibrium of \(Q\): \(\dfrac{q}{x^2} = \dfrac{9q}{(16 - x)^2}\); equilibrium of \(+q\): \(\dfrac{|Q|}{x^2} = \dfrac{9q}{16^2}\)
Step 1: \(16 - x = 3x \Rightarrow x = 4\) cm
Step 2: \(|Q| = \dfrac{9q \times 16}{256} = \dfrac{9q}{16}\)
Step 3: \(Q\) must pull \(+q\) towards it, against the push of \(+9q\), so \(Q\) is negative
Common mistake: \(+\dfrac{9q}{16}\) (A) gets the size right but not the sign; a positive \(Q\) would push both outer charges away.
Key point: Check the equilibrium of each charge, not only the middle one.

@@ P1|Potential on the axis of a charged ring|e|n|A|ring axial potential
A ring of radius 3 cm carries a charge of 10 nC spread uniformly. The potential at a point on its axis 4 cm from the centre is:
A) 1800 V
B) 3000 V
C) 2250 V
D) zero
=
Given: \(Q = 10^{-8}\) C, \(R = 0.03\) m, \(x = 0.04\) m
Formula: \(V = \dfrac{kQ}{\sqrt{R^2 + x^2}}\)
Step 1: \(\sqrt{R^2 + x^2} = 0.05\) m
Step 2: \(V = \dfrac{9 \times 10^9 \times 10^{-8}}{0.05} = 1800\) V
Common mistake: 3000 V (B) is the potential at the centre, \(\dfrac{kQ}{R}\). 2250 V (C) uses only \(x\).
Key point: Every bit of the ring is the same distance \(\sqrt{R^2 + x^2}\) from an axial point.

@@ P1|Surface charge densities of two connected spheres|m|n|D|connected spheres charge density
Two charged conducting spheres of radii 1 cm and 2 cm are joined by a thin wire. The ratio of the surface charge densities \(\dfrac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_2}\) (smaller to larger) is:

B) \(\dfrac{1}{4}\)
C) 4

Given: \(R_1 = 1\) cm, \(R_2 = 2\) cm; joined, so at the same potential

@@ P2|Microscopic picture of conduction — which hold|m|s|B|relaxation time temperature,electron density metal temperature,drift field proportional
Consider the following statements about conduction in a metal:

(B) The relaxation time decreases as the temperature rises.
(C) The drift velocity is proportional to the applied electric field.
(D) The number density of free electrons rises sharply with temperature.

B) (A), (B) and (C) only
