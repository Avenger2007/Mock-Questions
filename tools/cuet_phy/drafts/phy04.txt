Consider the following statements:
(A) Electric charge is quantised.
(B) The total charge of an isolated system is conserved.
(C) Charges add algebraically, like real numbers.
(D) A body can carry a charge of \(2.4 \times 10^{-19}\) C.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
A) (A) and (B) only
B) (A), (C) and (D) only
C) (A), (B) and (C) only
D) (A), (B), (C) and (D)
=
Why: (A) every charge is \(q = ne\). (B) charge is neither created nor destroyed. (C) charge is a scalar, so \(+2\) and \(-5\) units give \(-3\).

@@ P1|Field midway between equal and opposite charges|m|n|B|field midpoint unlike charges
Charges of \(+2\ \mu\text{C}\) and \(-2\ \mu\text{C}\) are 20 cm apart. The electric field at the midpoint between them is:
A) zero
B) \(3.6 \times 10^{6}\) N C⁻¹
C) \(1.8 \times 10^{6}\) N C⁻¹
D) \(9 \times 10^{5}\) N C⁻¹
=
Given: \(q = 2 \times 10^{-6}\) C, distance of each from the midpoint \(r = 0.1\) m
Formula: \(E = \dfrac{kq}{r^2}\) for each charge
Step 1: Each field: \(\dfrac{9 \times 10^9 \times 2 \times 10^{-6}}{0.01} = 1.8 \times 10^6\) N C⁻¹

@@ P1|Voltage across one of two capacitors in series|m|n|B|series capacitors voltage division

B) 6 V
C) 10 V

=
Given: \(C_1 = 2\ \mu\text{F}\), \(C_2 = 3\ \mu\text{F}\), \(V = 10\) V

@@ P1|Work done by the field round a closed path|m|d|A|closed path work electrostatic
The work done by an electrostatic field in moving a charge once around any closed path is:
A) zero
B) positive
C) negative
D) \(qE\) times the length of the path
=
Why: The electrostatic field is conservative: the work depends only on the end points, and on a closed path they coincide.
Why not the others: (B) and (C) would mean energy is gained or lost on returning to the start. (D) treats the field as uniform and along the path everywhere.
Key point: This is why a potential can be defined for the electrostatic field.

@@ P2|Drift velocity when a wire's length doubles at fixed voltage|m|d|C|drift velocity length fixed voltage

=
