@@ P1|What Gauss's law says about flux and enclosed charge — which hold|m|s|B|gauss law any distribution,outside charge zero flux

(B) A charge outside a closed surface gives zero net flux through it.
(C) The field \(\vec{E}\) at points on a Gaussian surface is due to all the charges, inside and outside.
(D) Gauss's law holds only for symmetric charge distributions.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
A) (A) and (B) only
B) (A), (B) and (C) only
C) (B), (C) and (D) only
D) (A), (B), (C) and (D)
=
Why: (A) \(\Phi = \dfrac{q_{enc}}{\varepsilon_0}\). (B) its field lines enter and leave the surface equally. (C) outside charges change \(\vec{E}\) on the surface but not the net flux.
Why not the others: (D) is false: Gauss's law holds for any distribution; symmetry only makes it easy to use for finding \(E\). Options (C) and (D) include (D); (A) leaves out (C).
Key point: Gauss's law is always true; it is only sometimes convenient.

@@ P1|Field at the free corner of a triangle with two equal charges|h|n|D|equilateral triangle two charges field
