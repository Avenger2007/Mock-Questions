@@ P1|Capacitor facts — which hold|m|s|D|capacitance independent of charge,series same charge parallel same voltage
Consider the following statements:
(A) The capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor does not depend on the charge placed on it.
(B) Filling the gap of a capacitor with a dielectric increases its capacitance.
(C) Capacitors joined in series all carry the same charge.
(D) Capacitors joined in parallel all have the same potential difference.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
A) (A) and (B) only
B) (A), (C) and (D) only
C) (B), (C) and (D) only
D) (A), (B), (C) and (D)
=
Why: (A) \(C = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0A}{d}\) depends only on geometry and the medium. (B) \(C = \dfrac{K\varepsilon_0A}{d}\) with \(K > 1\). (C) the same charge flows onto each plate in a series chain. (D) each capacitor in parallel is connected across the same two points.
Why not the others: All four statements are true, so every option that leaves one out is wrong.
Key point: Doubling \(Q\) doubles \(V\); the ratio \(C = \dfrac{Q}{V}\) stays the same.

@@ P1|Four capacitor arrangements in increasing order of capacitance|m|q|C|capacitance arrangements order

@@ P1|Work done in moving a charge between two potentials|e|n|A|work done charge potential difference
A charge of \(2\ \mu\text{C}\) is moved from a point at 10 V to a point at 50 V. The work done against the field is:
A) \(80\ \mu\text{J}\)
B) \(120\ \mu\text{J}\)
C) \(20\ \mu\text{J}\)
D) \(100\ \mu\text{J}\)
=
Given: \(q = 2 \times 10^{-6}\) C, \(V_A = 10\) V, \(V_B = 50\) V
Formula: \(W = q(V_B - V_A)\)
Step 1: \(V_B - V_A = 40\) V
Step 2: \(W = 2 \times 10^{-6} \times 40 = 8 \times 10^{-5}\) J \(= 80\ \mu\text{J}\)
Common mistake: \(120\ \mu\text{J}\) (B) adds the two potentials. \(100\ \mu\text{J}\) (D) uses only the final potential.
Key point: Work depends on the potential difference, not on the path.

A) \(18\ \mu\text{F}\)

C) \(2\ \mu\text{F}\)

@@ P1|Energy stored after a dielectric enters an isolated capacitor|h|n|D|dielectric isolated capacitor energy
A \(4\ \mu\text{F}\) air capacitor is charged to 100 V and then disconnected from the battery. A dielectric of \(K = 2\) is then made to fill the gap completely. The energy now stored is:
A) 40 mJ
B) 20 mJ
C) 5 mJ
D) 10 mJ
=
Given: \(C_0 = 4\ \mu\text{F}\), \(V_0 = 100\) V, \(K = 2\); charge fixed
Formula: \(U = \dfrac{Q^2}{2C}\)
Step 1: \(Q = 4 \times 10^{-6} \times 100 = 4 \times 10^{-4}\) C; \(U_0 = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 10^{-6} \times 10^4 = 20\) mJ
Step 2: \(C = 8\ \mu\text{F}\), so \(U = \dfrac{U_0}{K} = 10\) mJ
Common mistake: 40 mJ (A) keeps \(V\) fixed and uses \(U = \dfrac{1}{2}CV^2\), which applies only while the battery stays connected.
Key point: Isolated capacitor: \(Q\) fixed, so energy falls \(K\) times.

@@ P1|Why electrostatic field lines never close on themselves|m|d|A|conservative field no closed loops
Electrostatic field lines never form closed loops because:
A) the electrostatic field is conservative
B) two field lines can never cross each other
C) electric charge is always quantised
D) the field inside a conductor is zero
=
Why: Along a closed field line the field would do non-zero work on a charge going round it; for a conservative field the work round any closed path is zero, so such a loop cannot exist.
Why not the others: (B) is true but a separate property. (C) and (D) are true facts unrelated to the shape of field lines.
Key point: Electrostatic field lines start on positive charges and end on negative ones.

@@ P2|Kirchhoff's rules and resistivity — which claims fail|m|s|B|kirchhoff rules conservation,resistivity length independent
Consider the following statements:
(A) Kirchhoff's junction rule follows from the conservation of charge.
(B) Kirchhoff's loop rule follows from the conservation of momentum.
(C) The resistivity of a wire does not depend on its length.
(D) The resistivity of a metal increases as its temperature rises.
Which of the statements given above is/are NOT correct?
A) (A) only
B) (B) only
C) (C) only
D) (D) only
=
Why: Only (B) is false: the loop rule follows from the conservation of energy (the electrostatic field is conservative).
Why not the others: (A) charge does not pile up at a junction. (C) resistivity is a property of the material; resistance depends on length. (D) more vibrating ions mean more collisions. Options (A), (C) and (D) name a true statement as false.
Key point: Junction rule: charge; loop rule: energy.

@@ P2|Resistance of a wire at a higher temperature|m|n|C|temperature coefficient resistance
A wire has resistance \(10\ \Omega\) at \(20^\circ\)C. Its temperature coefficient of resistance is \(4 \times 10^{-3}\ ^\circ\text{C}^{-1}\). Its resistance at \(70^\circ\)C is:
A) \(14\ \Omega\)
B) \(10.2\ \Omega\)
C) \(12\ \Omega\)
D) \(2\ \Omega\)
=
Given: \(R_0 = 10\ \Omega\), \(\alpha = 4 \times 10^{-3}\ ^\circ\text{C}^{-1}\), \(\Delta T = 50^\circ\)C
Formula: \(R = R_0(1 + \alpha\Delta T)\)

Step 2: \(R = 10 \times 1.2 = 12\ \Omega\)

Key point: Use the change in temperature, not the final temperature.

@@ P2|Heat from a 55 Ω heater on 220 V in 10 minutes|m|n|A|joule heating time

@@ P2|Current from four cells in series|m|n|D|cells in series internal resistance

@@ P2|Equivalent resistance of a balanced bridge network|h|n|B|balanced bridge equivalent resistance
In a bridge network, the arms are \(P = 2\ \Omega\), \(Q = 4\ \Omega\), \(R = 3\ \Omega\) and \(S = 6\ \Omega\), and a \(5\ \Omega\) resistor joins the junction of \(P\) and \(Q\) to the junction of \(R\) and \(S\). The equivalent resistance between the input corners is:
A) \(20\ \Omega\)
B) \(3.6\ \Omega\)
C) \(2.4\ \Omega\)
D) \(1.6\ \Omega\)
=
Given: \(\dfrac{P}{Q} = \dfrac{2}{4} = \dfrac{R}{S} = \dfrac{3}{6}\), so the bridge is balanced
Formula: balanced bridge: the middle resistor carries no current and can be removed
Step 1: Upper branch \(P + Q = 6\ \Omega\); lower branch \(R + S = 9\ \Omega\)
Step 2: \(R_{eq} = \dfrac{6 \times 9}{6 + 9} = 3.6\ \Omega\)

@@ P2|SI unit of electron mobility|e|d|D|mobility si unit
The SI unit of the mobility of charge carriers is:

@@ P3|Field at the centre of a semicircular wire|m|n|A|semicircle field centre

@@ P3|Torque on a coil whose plane is at 60° to the field|h|n|B|coil torque plane angle

@@ P3|Force on a charge moving along the field|e|d|A|force charge parallel field
A charged particle moves parallel to a uniform magnetic field. The magnetic force on it is:
A) zero
B) \(qvB\)
C) \(\dfrac{qvB}{2}\)
D) \(2qvB\)
=
Why: \(F = qvB\sin\theta\) and \(\theta = 0^\circ\), so \(F = 0\); the particle keeps moving in a straight line.

@@ P4|Transformer facts — which hold|m|s|C|transformer mutual induction dc,step down current
Consider the following statements about an ideal transformer:

B) (A), (B) and (C) only
