@@ P1|Speed gained by a charge repelled to a great distance|h|n|C|charge released repelled far speed
A particle of mass 2 g carrying \(+2\ \mu\text{C}\) is released from rest 10 cm from a fixed charge of \(+10\ \mu\text{C}\). Ignoring gravity, its speed when it is very far away is about:
A) 30 m s⁻¹
B) 60 m s⁻¹
C) 42.4 m s⁻¹
D) 1.34 m s⁻¹
=
Given: \(q = 2 \times 10^{-6}\) C, \(Q = 10^{-5}\) C, \(r = 0.1\) m, \(m = 2 \times 10^{-3}\) kg
Formula: \(\dfrac{kqQ}{r} = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2\) (all the potential energy becomes kinetic)
Step 1: \(U = \dfrac{9 \times 10^9 \times 2 \times 10^{-6} \times 10^{-5}}{0.1} = 1.8\) J
Step 2: \(v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 \times 1.8}{2 \times 10^{-3}}} = \sqrt{1800} \approx 42.4\) m s⁻¹
Common mistake: 30 m s⁻¹ (A) drops the \(\dfrac{1}{2}\) in the kinetic energy.
Key point: Far away the potential energy is zero, so all of it has become kinetic energy.
