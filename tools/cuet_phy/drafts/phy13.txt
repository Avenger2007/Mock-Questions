@@ P1|Charge and field on conductors and electrostatic shielding — which hold|m|s|A|surface density varies curvature,cavity shielding zero field
Consider the following statements:
(A) The electric field inside an empty cavity in a conductor is zero.
(B) Excess charge on a conductor resides on its outer surface.
(C) Just outside a charged conductor, the field is \(\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}\) and normal to the surface.
(D) The surface charge density is the same everywhere on an irregularly shaped conductor.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
A) (A), (B) and (C) only
B) (A) and (B) only
C) (B), (C) and (D) only
D) (A), (B), (C) and (D)
=
Why: (A) this is electrostatic shielding. (B) charges repel to the outer surface. (C) a tangential part would move charges along the surface.
Why not the others: (D) is false: charge crowds where the surface is sharply curved, so \(\sigma\) is largest at points. Options (C) and (D) include (D); (B) leaves out (C).
Key point: Sharp points carry the highest surface charge density.

@@ P1|Charge on a capacitor in a d.c. circuit at steady state|h|n|C|capacitor steady state dc circuit

A) \(60\ \mu\text{C}\)
B) \(40\ \mu\text{C}\)
C) \(20\ \mu\text{C}\)
D) zero
=
Given: steady state, so no current flows into the capacitor
Formula: \(I = \dfrac{V}{R_{total}}\); \(Q = CV_{2\Omega}\)
