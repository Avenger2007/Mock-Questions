# CUET Political Science Test 7 — odd key 13/12/13/12. Passage: I4 (Nehru's foreign policy and NAM).

%P I4
Jawaharlal Nehru, who shaped independent India's foreign policy, set it three broad aims: to preserve the sovereignty won after a long struggle, to protect the country's territorial integrity, and to promote rapid economic development. He chose non-alignment as the way to achieve them. Non-alignment did not mean staying away from world affairs or being neutral on every question. It meant keeping out of the military alliances of both Cold War blocs and judging each issue on its merits. Even before Independence, India hosted the Asian Relations Conference in New Delhi in March 1947. It backed Indonesia's struggle for freedom from Dutch rule and in 1949 convened an international conference in its support. With leaders such as Tito of Yugoslavia, Nasser of Egypt, Sukarno of Indonesia and Nkrumah of Ghana, Nehru went on to found the Non-Aligned Movement. Staying outside the blocs also let India receive economic and technical help from both the United States and the Soviet Union. Critics complained that non-alignment was unprincipled and that India often avoided taking a firm stand. Its defenders replied that it gave a newly independent and poor country the freedom to decide for itself.
