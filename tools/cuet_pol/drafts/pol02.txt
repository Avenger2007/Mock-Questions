# CUET Political Science Test 2 — even key 12/13/12/13. Passage: W6 (global commons and common property resources).

%P W6
Some parts of the world lie outside the sovereign jurisdiction of any one state. They are called the global commons, and they include the earth's atmosphere, Antarctica, the ocean floor beyond national waters and outer space. Because no single state controls them, they can be managed only through international cooperation. That cooperation has produced landmark agreements: a treaty that reserves Antarctica for peaceful and scientific use, a protocol to protect the ozone layer, and later an environmental protocol for the Antarctic region. Yet agreement is hard to reach. Scientific knowledge about problems such as ozone depletion is often uncertain and disputed, and the benefits of exploiting the commons depend heavily on technology, which the industrialised North has and the developing South largely lacks. Below the global level, many communities manage resources collectively. A common property resource is owned and used by a group whose members have both rights and duties regarding its care. Customary rules and mutual understanding, rather than the market or the state, keep such resources alive, though privatisation, rising population and the pressure of the market have weakened many of these arrangements.
