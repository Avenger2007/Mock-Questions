# CUET Political Science Test 14 — even key 12/13/12/13. Passage: W5 (human and cooperative security).

%P W5
For most of modern history, security meant protecting a state against military attack from outside. Thinking on security has since widened. The idea of human security places people, rather than territories or governments, at the centre. Some scholars would limit it to violent threats against individuals. Others would add hunger, disease and natural disasters, which kill far more people than wars do. The broadest version speaks of 'freedom from want' and 'freedom from fear'. New sources of insecurity have also drawn attention. Global poverty is one: the gap between rich and poor countries leaves millions exposed to risks they cannot control. Health epidemics are another. By 2003, about 4 crore people across the world were infected with HIV-AIDS, two-thirds of them in Africa. Diseases of this kind do not stop at national frontiers, and no single government can contain them on its own. For this reason, dealing with such threats calls for cooperation. Cooperative security may involve international organisations such as the UN and the WHO, non-governmental organisations, business corporations and even eminent persons. It may be bilateral, regional, continental or global, depending on the nature of the threat.
