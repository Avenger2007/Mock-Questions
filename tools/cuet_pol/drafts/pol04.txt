# CUET Political Science Test 4 — even key 12/13/12/13. Passage: W1 (shock therapy in the post-communist states).

%P W1
After the collapse of communism, Russia, the Central Asian republics and the countries of Eastern Europe set out on a painful transition from an authoritarian socialist system to a democratic capitalist one. The model they followed, shaped by the World Bank and the IMF, aimed to turn each economy into a capitalist one quickly: state ownership gave way to private property, collective farms were replaced by private farming, and economies were opened to free trade and foreign investment. In Russia about ninety per cent of industrial companies were put up for sale, often to well-connected buyers at throwaway prices, in what was called the largest garage sale in history. The value of the rouble fell sharply, and high inflation wiped out people's savings. The old system of social welfare was dismantled, a new class of rich oligarchs emerged, and economic inequality widened. Politics changed in a hurry too. New constitutions were drafted quickly, and many countries, including Russia, gave strong powers to an executive president while parliaments remained weak. Over time some of these economies recovered, partly through the export of natural resources such as oil and gas.
