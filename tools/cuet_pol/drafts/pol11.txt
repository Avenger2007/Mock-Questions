# CUET Political Science Test 11 — odd key 13/12/13/12. Passage: I1 (the first general election, 1951–52).

%P I1
India's first general election was a task without precedent. The country had to draw the boundaries of constituencies, prepare electoral rolls for every adult and train lakhs of officials, and the election had to be postponed twice before it could be held. Voting finally took place over several months, between October 1951 and February 1952. Only about 15 per cent of the voters were literate, so special methods had to be found to let people who could not read cast their votes. Many observers, at home and abroad, doubted that a poor and largely illiterate country could choose its government through the ballot. One editor called the exercise the biggest gamble in history, and some predicted that the country would soon fall back on rule by a strong leader or the army. Yet the election was held peacefully, more than half of the eligible voters took part, and the results were accepted as fair even by those who lost. The experience showed that democracy could take root in conditions that many had thought unsuitable, and it became a landmark in the history of democracy all over the world.
