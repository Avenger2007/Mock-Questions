# CUET Political Science Test 10 — even key 12/13/12/13. Passage: W4 (reforming the Security Council).

%P W4
The structure of the UN Security Council still reflects the distribution of power in the world as it stood in 1945. Since then the membership of the United Nations has grown from 51 to 193 states, many of them newly independent countries of Asia, Africa and Latin America, yet the Council has been enlarged only once. Critics say that the developing world is poorly represented in the body that takes the most important decisions on peace and security. Proposals for reform include adding new permanent members, adding more non-permanent members, or both, and there is a long debate about which criteria should decide who deserves a seat. The veto is an even harder question: some want it abolished or limited, while the permanent members are unwilling to give it up. India argues that, as the world's largest democracy, home to about one-fifth of humanity, with a growing economy and a long record in peacekeeping, it should be a permanent member. Any change, however, requires an amendment to the UN Charter, which must be approved by two-thirds of the members, including all five permanent members. This is why, despite years of debate, the Council looks much as it did decades ago.
