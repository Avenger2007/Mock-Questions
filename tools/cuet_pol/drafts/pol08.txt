# CUET Political Science Test 8 — even key 12/13/12/13. Passage: W3 (the ethnic conflict in Sri Lanka).

%P W3
Sri Lanka has kept a democratic political system since it became independent in 1948, but it has faced a long and bitter ethnic conflict. Its politics came to be dominated by forces representing the Sinhala majority, and some argued that the island should belong to the Sinhalas alone. Tamils, both those long settled in the north and east and those whose families had come from India as plantation workers, felt neglected, and Tamil nationalism turned militant. From 1983 the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam waged an armed struggle for a separate Tamil state in the north and east. Tamils in India pressed the Indian government to act. In 1987 India signed an accord with Sri Lanka and sent a peacekeeping force, but the force was drawn into fighting with the militants and withdrew in 1989 without achieving its aim. Later, Norway and Iceland tried to bring the two sides to the negotiating table. Despite the conflict, Sri Lanka recorded considerable economic growth and high levels of human development. It was among the first developing countries to control the growth of its population and the first country in South Asia to liberalise its economy. The armed conflict ended in 2009 with the defeat of the militants.
