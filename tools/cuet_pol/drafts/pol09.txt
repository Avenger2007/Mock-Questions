# CUET Political Science Test 9 — odd key 13/12/13/12. Passage: I8 (Mandal and the coalition era).

%P I8
The ninth general election, held in 1989, brought an end to what scholars had called the 'Congress system'. The Congress, which had won a record number of seats only five years earlier, was defeated, and no party won a majority on its own. A National Front government was formed with outside support from parties on both the right and the left. In August 1990 this government decided to implement a recommendation of the Mandal Commission, officially the Second Backward Classes Commission, which had been appointed to identify the socially and educationally backward classes and to suggest ways of advancing them. The decision set off violent protests against reservations in many cities of north India, but it also helped parties that drew their support from the backward classes to grow stronger. For many years afterwards, no single party could form a government at the Centre on its own strength. National parties had to seek alliances with regional and caste-based parties, and governments came to depend on partners who could bargain hard for their own interests. Some saw this as instability; others saw it as a sign that more groups now had a voice in power.
