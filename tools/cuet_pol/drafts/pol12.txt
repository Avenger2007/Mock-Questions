# CUET Political Science Test 12 — even key 12/13/12/13. Passage: W7 (resistance to globalisation and the World Social Forum).

%P W7
Globalisation has provoked strong criticism. Critics on the left argue that it makes the rich richer and the poor poorer, both within countries and between them. They point out that when the state withdraws from welfare, the poorest lose the protection they once had. Others worry that local traditions and ways of life will be swamped by outside influences. Yet most movements of resistance do not reject global contacts altogether. What they oppose is a particular programme of globalisation, one that they see as driven by powerful states and corporations for their own benefit. Interestingly, these movements make full use of the tools that globalisation itself has created. Activists in different countries share ideas over the internet, coordinate protests across borders and meet at international gatherings. The World Social Forum, which first met in 2001, is one such gathering. It is not a political party and does not contest elections; rather, it is an open meeting place where human rights activists, environmentalists, labour groups, youth and women's organisations discuss alternatives. In this sense, the resistance to globalisation has itself become global.
