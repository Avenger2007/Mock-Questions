# CUET Political Science Test 5 — odd key 13/12/13/12. Passage: I7 (Punjab and the Rajiv–Longowal Accord).

%P I7
The Akali Dal, formed in 1920 as the political wing of the Sikhs, had led the movement for a Punjabi-speaking State, and in 1966 a smaller Punjab was carved out of the old State. In the 1970s a section of the Akalis began to press for greater autonomy for the region, and by the early 1980s armed militants had made the Golden Temple at Amritsar their base. In June 1984 the Government of India sent the army into the shrine to flush them out. The operation succeeded in its military aim but deeply hurt Sikh sentiment, and it was followed by Indira Gandhi's assassination and violence against Sikhs in Delhi and elsewhere. After the 1984 elections, Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi opened talks with moderate Akali leaders. In July 1985 he and Harchand Singh Longowal signed an agreement. It provided that Chandigarh would be transferred to Punjab, that a separate commission would settle the border dispute with Haryana, that a tribunal would decide how the waters of the Ravi and Beas would be shared among Punjab, Haryana and Rajasthan, that people affected by militancy would be compensated, and that the Armed Forces Special Powers Act would be withdrawn from Punjab. Peace did not come quickly. When elections were held in 1992, only about a quarter of the voters turned out. Normal political life returned slowly, and by the middle of the 1990s Punjab had moved back towards peace.
