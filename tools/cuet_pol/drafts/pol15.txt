# CUET Political Science Test 15 — odd key 13/12/13/12. Passage: I3 (the early Five Year Plans).

%P I3
Once the Planning Commission had been set up, the central question was what the plans should give priority to. The First Five Year Plan gave the highest priority to agriculture. Large sums were invested in dams and irrigation, and the Bhakra Nangal project was among the most ambitious of these. The planners also saw land reform as the key to rural development. The Second Plan, drawn up under the guidance of P.C. Mahalanobis, shifted the emphasis to heavy industry, in the belief that rapid industrialisation would pull the whole economy forward. This choice did not go unchallenged. Some critics argued that the plans neglected agriculture and had no clear strategy for rural India. Others complained that the public sector grew too powerful and created vested interests of its own, and that the system of licences and permits held back private enterprise. Yet even critics accept that these early plans laid the foundations of India's later growth. The large irrigation and power projects, the steel plants and the heavy industries built in this period served the country for decades.
