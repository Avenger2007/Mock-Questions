# CUET Political Science Test 3 — odd key 13/12/13/12. Passage: I2 (integration of the princely states).

%P I2
At Independence, British India contained more than five hundred princely states, each ruled by a prince who had accepted British paramountcy. When paramountcy lapsed in 1947, these rulers became legally free to join India or Pakistan, or to remain independent. The interim government took a firm but flexible line. Three considerations guided it: most people in the princely states clearly wanted to be part of India; the government was willing to give some regions a measure of autonomy; and, coming after Partition, the unity of the country's territory had become supremely important. Through persuasion, almost every ruler signed a document by which his state agreed to become part of India. A few cases proved harder. The Nizam of Hyderabad wanted independence and signed only a temporary arrangement with India while a people's movement grew against him; in September 1948 the Indian Army moved in and the state was integrated. In Manipur the Maharaja held elections to an assembly on the basis of universal adult franchise, but in September 1949 the Government of India pressed him to sign a merger agreement without consulting that popularly elected assembly, which caused lasting resentment.
