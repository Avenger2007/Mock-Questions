# CUET Political Science Test 13 — odd key 13/12/13/12. Passage: I5 (the Congress split of 1969).

%P I5
In 1967 the Congress Working Committee adopted a Ten Point Programme. It included social control of banks, nationalisation of general insurance, a ceiling on urban property and income, public distribution of food grains, land reforms and the provision of house sites to the rural poor. The senior leaders of the party formally approved the programme, but many of them had serious reservations about it. The tension between the Prime Minister and these leaders came into the open in 1969, when the office of the President of India fell vacant on the death of Dr Zakir Hussain. Despite Indira Gandhi's reservations, the party leadership chose N. Sanjeeva Reddy, a long-time political rival of hers, as the official candidate. Indira Gandhi then encouraged the Vice-President, V.V. Giri, to file his nomination as an independent candidate. She also asked the party's members of Parliament and the legislatures to cast a 'conscience vote', that is, to vote as their own judgement told them rather than follow the party's choice. Giri won by a narrow margin. The defeat of the official candidate formalised the split in the party. Indira Gandhi presented the struggle as an ideological one, between progressives who stood for the poor and conservatives who stood for the rich, and this gave her a popular image.
