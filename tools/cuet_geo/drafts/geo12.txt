Watershed management means the efficient management and conservation of surface water and groundwater. It involves preventing runoff, and storing and recharging groundwater through percolation tanks, recharge wells and similar methods. Haryali, a watershed development project sponsored by the Central Government, aims to enable the rural population to conserve water for drinking, irrigation, fisheries and afforestation; it is carried out by Gram Panchayats with people's participation. Neeru-Meeru (Water and You) in Andhra Pradesh and the Arvary Pani Sansad in Alwar, Rajasthan, have built percolation tanks, dug-out ponds (johads) and check dams through people's participation.

In Village V (illustrative), a house has a roof of 200 sq m, the area receives 600 mm of rain a year, and about 80 per cent of the rain falling on the roof can be collected.

@@ B4|Water harvested from a roof|m|p|C|rooftop rwh case calculation
How much rainwater can the house in Village V collect from its roof in a year?

B) 1,200 litres

=

@@ A2|Actual change in population|m|n|D|components of population change
