Metro M (an illustrative city) has a population of 2 crore. About 40 lakh people live in its slums; most of them are migrants from rural areas who work in low-paid, unorganised jobs. The city produces about 12,000 tonnes of solid waste a day, of which only 9,000 tonnes are collected. The rest piles up in lanes, open drains and vacant plots, and untreated sewage flows into the river that runs through the city.

@@ B9|Slum dwellers as a share of the city|e|p|C|slum share calculation
What share of Metro M's population lives in slums?
A) 40 per cent
B) 2 per cent
C) 20 per cent
D) 5 per cent
=
Given: slum population = 40 lakh; city population = 2 crore = 200 lakh.
Step 1: Share = 40 ÷ 200 × 100.
Step 2: = 20 per cent.
Common mistake: reading 40 lakh as 40 per cent (option A), or dividing 200 by 40 (5, option D).

@@ B9|Uncollected urban waste|m|p|A|uncollected waste calculation
What share of the solid waste produced in Metro M each day is left uncollected?
A) 25 per cent
B) 75 per cent
C) 33 per cent
D) 3 per cent
=
Given: waste produced = 12,000 tonnes a day; collected = 9,000 tonnes.
Step 1: Uncollected = 12,000 − 9,000 = 3,000 tonnes.
Step 2: 3,000 ÷ 12,000 × 100 = 25 per cent.
Common mistake: giving the collected share (75 per cent, option B), or dividing by the collected amount (about 33 per cent, option C).

B) 8 cubic metres
C) 1,250 cubic metres
D) 80 cubic metres
=
Given: usable water = 1,200 billion m³ = 1,20,000 crore m³; population = 150 crore.
Step 1: Per capita availability = water ÷ population.
Step 2: = 1,20,000 crore ÷ 150 crore = 800 cubic metres per person.
Common mistake: dividing population by water (option C is 150 ÷ 1,200 × 10,000), or slipping a zero (80, option D).
