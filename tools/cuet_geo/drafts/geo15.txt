Every port serves an area, called its hinterland, from which it receives goods for export and to which it sends imported goods. India's ports differ in their sites and in the size of their hinterlands. Kolkata is an inland riverine port on the Hugli, about 128 km from the Bay of Bengal. Its hinterland covers much of the Ganga–Brahmaputra basin, but the river channel silts up, so Haldia was developed downstream to ease congestion. Mumbai has a spacious, well-sheltered natural harbour, and the Jawaharlal Nehru Port was built at Nhava Sheva as a satellite port to decongest it. Visakhapatnam is a landlocked harbour, linked to the sea by a channel cut through solid rock and sand. Chennai, one of the oldest ports on the east coast, has an artificial harbour built in 1859; it is not well suited to large ships because the water near the coast is shallow, and Ennore was built to its north to relieve the pressure on it.
