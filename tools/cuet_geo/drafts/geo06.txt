Trans-continental railways run across a continent and link its two ends. They were built for economic and political reasons, to develop long runs in different directions.

The Trans-Siberian Railway is the most important rail route in Asia and the longest double-tracked and electrified trans-continental railway in the world, about 9,332 km long. It runs across Russia from the Baltic coast in the west to the Pacific coast in the east, passing through Moscow, Ufa, Novosibirsk, Irkutsk, Chita and Khabarovsk, and crosses the Ural mountains and the Ob and Yenisei rivers. It has helped to open up the Asian region to West European markets.

The Trans-Canadian Railway, about 7,050 km long, was built in 1886, initially as part of an agreement to bring British Columbia into the Canadian federation. It runs from the Atlantic coast to the Pacific coast, through Montreal, Ottawa, Winnipeg and Calgary, and links the Quebec–Montreal industrial region with the wheat belt of the Prairies and the coniferous forest region to the north.

Other trans-continental routes cross the USA, Australia and Europe.
