The Indira Gandhi Canal, earlier called the Rajasthan Canal, is one of the largest canal systems in India. Conceived in 1948 and launched in 1958, it takes off from a barrage built near the confluence of the Sutlej and the Beas in Punjab and runs roughly parallel to the Pakistan border, at an average distance of about 40 km, through the Thar Desert of western Rajasthan.

The command area was developed in two stages. Stage I covers Ganganagar, Hanumangarh and the northern part of Bikaner district, where the land is gently undulating. Stage II covers parts of Bikaner, Jaisalmer, Barmer, Jodhpur, Nagaur and Churu, a desert of shifting sand dunes where summer temperatures can reach 50°C; here part of the water is carried by lift canals.

Canal water has spread intensive cultivation of wheat, cotton, groundnut and rice, but heavy irrigation has also caused waterlogging and soil salinity in places. Planners now stress strict water management, command area development works on farms, reclamation of salt-affected land and eco-development through afforestation, shelterbelts and pastures.

@@ A2|Natural increase from crude birth and death rates|m|n|A|natural growth from cbr and cdr

C) 7.4 lakh

=
Given: population = 2,00,00,000; CBR = 28 per thousand; CDR = 9 per thousand; no migration.

@@ B3|Cropping intensity|m|n|B|cropping intensity

A) 40 per cent
B) 140 per cent
C) 71.4 per cent
D) 160 per cent
=
Given: net sown area (NSA) = 140 lakh ha; area sown more than once = 56 lakh ha.
Step 1: Gross cropped area (GCA) = 140 + 56 = 196 lakh ha.
Step 2: Cropping intensity = GCA ÷ NSA × 100 = 196 ÷ 140 × 100 = 140 per cent.
