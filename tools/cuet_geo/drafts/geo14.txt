Industries try to maximise profits by cutting costs, so they are best located where the cost of production is lowest. Many factors influence that choice. The existence of a market is the most important: developed regions such as Europe, North America, Japan and Australia offer large global markets because people there have high purchasing power, while the densely populated regions of South and South-east Asia also provide large markets. Industries that use cheap, bulky and weight-losing raw materials, such as ores, are located close to their sources; so are those that use perishable raw materials, as in agro-processing and dairy products. Labour still matters, despite growing mechanisation and automation. Industries that use large amounts of power, such as aluminium smelting, are located close to sources of energy. Efficient transport and communication, government policies that promote balanced regional development, and agglomeration economies also shape where industries are located.
