# CUET Agriculture Test 14 — even key 12/13/12/13. Passage: L4 (composite fish culture).

%P L4
Composite fish culture is a system in which several compatible species of fish are reared together in one pond, so that every feeding zone and every kind of natural food is used. It usually combines the three Indian major carps with three exotic carps. Catla feeds at the surface on zooplankton, rohu feeds in the middle (column) layer on plant matter, and mrigal is a bottom feeder that eats decaying organic matter. Among the exotic carps, silver carp feeds at the surface on phytoplankton, grass carp eats aquatic weeds and other vegetation, and common carp is a bottom-dwelling omnivore. Because the species compete little for food, yields of 4–5 tonnes per hectare a year are possible. Before stocking, unwanted weeds are cleared and predatory and weed fish are killed with mahua oil cake, which later breaks down and acts as manure; lime and cow dung are then applied to correct acidity and boost natural food. Fingerlings are stocked at about 5,000 per hectare, and a common plan gives catla a 30 percent share, dividing the rest among the other five species. The fish are fed rice bran and oilcake as a supplement and harvested after about a year. Seed of these carps is now produced in hatcheries by induced breeding, in which a hormone (pituitary extract or a synthetic one) is injected to make the fish spawn.
