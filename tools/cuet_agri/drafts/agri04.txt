# CUET Agriculture Test 4 — even key 12/13/12/13. Passage: A2 (hybridization and the semi-dwarf wheats).

%P A2
Hybridization is the crossing of two genetically different plants to bring their good qualities together in one variety. In a self-pollinated crop such as wheat, the breeder first chooses parents that complement each other, for example one with high yield and another with disease resistance. The anthers of the flowers of the female parent are removed before they shed pollen; this is called emasculation. The emasculated spikes are covered with bags so that no stray pollen can reach them. When the stigmas are ready, pollen from the chosen male parent is dusted on them, the spikes are bagged again and labelled. The F₁ plants that grow from the crossed seed are uniform, but in the F₂ generation the genes segregate and the plants vary widely. The breeder selects the best plants in the F₂ and keeps selecting in later generations until the lines breed true. In the 1950s Norman Borlaug and his team in Mexico crossed local wheats with lines carrying dwarfing genes from the Japanese variety Norin 10. The new semi-dwarf wheats had short, stiff straw, so they did not fall over (lodge) when heavily fertilized and irrigated, and they could turn extra nitrogen into grain rather than straw. In the mid-1960s India imported seed of these wheats, and Indian breeders, encouraged by M.S. Swaminathan, selected varieties such as Kalyan Sona and Sonalika from the material. Wheat output rose rapidly, and in 1970 Borlaug received the Nobel Peace Prize for his work against hunger.
