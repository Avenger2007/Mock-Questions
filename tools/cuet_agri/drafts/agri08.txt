# CUET Agriculture Test 8 — even key 12/13/12/13. Passage: A1 (the monsoon and agromet advisories).

%P A1
For most Indian farmers the monsoon decides the fate of the kharif season. Yet the monsoon is rarely "normal" in every district: its onset may be late, rain may stop for two or three weeks in a dry spell, or a few days of very heavy rain may flood low fields. To help farmers deal with this uncertainty, the India Meteorological Department, working with agricultural universities and Krishi Vigyan Kendras, prepares agromet advisories for each district. These bulletins are issued twice a week. They give a forecast for the next few days of rainfall, maximum and minimum temperature, humidity, wind speed and cloud cover, and turn the forecast into practical advice. If heavy rain is expected, farmers are told to postpone spraying pesticides and fertilizers, because rain would wash them off the leaves, and to drain fields where water may stand. If a dry spell is forecast, they are advised to give a light irrigation if possible, to mulch the soil and to delay top dressing. When a mature crop faces a forecast of rain, harvesting is advanced and the produce is moved to safe storage. When the monsoon arrives very late, contingency plans suggest sowing short-duration varieties or switching to hardier crops such as pulses and millets. The advisories reach farmers by SMS, mobile apps, radio and newspapers. Studies show that farmers who follow them cut their losses and save money on inputs that would otherwise be wasted.
