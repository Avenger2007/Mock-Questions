# CUET Agriculture Test 11 — odd key 13/12/13/12. Passage: A4 (soil microbes and biofertilizers).

%P A4
Biofertilizers are preparations of living microorganisms that add nutrients to the soil or make them more available to plants. *Rhizobium* lives in the root nodules of legumes and fixes atmospheric nitrogen. Inside an effective nodule, a pigment called leghaemoglobin gives a pink colour and protects the nitrogen-fixing enzyme from oxygen. *Azotobacter* is free-living, while *Azospirillum* lives in close association with the roots of cereals such as maize and sorghum. Phosphate-solubilising bacteria (PSB), such as *Bacillus megaterium* and *Pseudomonas striata*, release organic acids that dissolve insoluble phosphates. The cultures are mixed with a carrier such as lignite or charcoal powder and sold in packets. For seed treatment, about 200 g of culture is used for 10 kg of seed. The culture is mixed with a cooled jaggery solution that helps it stick, and the seeds are coated evenly, dried in the shade and sown soon after. Direct sunlight kills the bacteria, so packets are stored in a cool place and used before the expiry date. If the seed also needs a chemical fungicide, the fungicide is applied first and the biofertilizer afterwards; the two are never mixed together. Biofertilizers are cheap and eco-friendly, but they act slowly and cannot fully replace chemical fertilizers.
