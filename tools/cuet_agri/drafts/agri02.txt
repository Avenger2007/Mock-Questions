# CUET Agriculture Test 2 — even key 12/13/12/13. Passage: L1 (indigenous cattle and the White Revolution).

%P L1
India's native cattle belong to the humped zebu type, *Bos indicus*. They are well suited to hot climates: they have loose skin, many sweat glands and a natural resistance to ticks and several tropical diseases, and they can live on coarse fodder. Breeds are grouped by use. Gir from the Saurashtra region of Gujarat and Red Sindhi are known for milk. Hallikar and Amritmahal of Karnataka are valued for draught power. Tharparkar from the Thar desert and Kankrej from north Gujarat are dual-purpose: the cows give fair amounts of milk and the bullocks work well. Because most native cows give only modest yields, exotic dairy breeds such as Holstein Friesian and Jersey were crossed with them. The crossbred cows give much more milk, but they suffer more in hot weather and need better feed and care. In 1970 the National Dairy Development Board launched Operation Flood. It spread the Anand pattern, in which village milk societies send milk to a district union, and the unions form a state federation that markets the milk and its products. Farmers own the whole chain and receive most of the consumer's rupee. Within three decades India became the world's largest producer of milk. In 2014 the government began the Rashtriya Gokul Mission to conserve and improve indigenous breeds, so that their hardiness is not lost in the rush to cross them with exotic bulls.
