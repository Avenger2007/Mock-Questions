# CUET Agriculture Test 6 — even key 12/13/12/13. Passage: L2 (managing a commercial layer flock).

%P L2
A commercial layer farm keeps hens for one purpose: a steady supply of eggs at low cost. Most farms rear hybrid strains developed from the White Leghorn, which are light-bodied and lay white eggs. Chicks are brooded under heat for their first weeks and then grown on a grower ration. The pullets usually begin to lay at about 18 to 20 weeks of age and reach peak production a few weeks later. A good hen lays about 300 eggs in her first laying year. Light has a strong effect on laying. Day length is gradually increased once laying begins and is then held at about 16 hours of light a day with electric bulbs; it is never allowed to shorten during the laying period, because shorter days reduce egg production. Each hen eats roughly 110 grams of feed a day. The layer ration contains much more calcium than the grower ration, because each eggshell is made mainly of calcium carbonate. Many farms house layers in cages, which keep the eggs clean and make it easy to watch each bird, while others use the deep litter system. Birds are vaccinated against diseases such as Ranikhet disease and Marek's disease, and clean water is always available. The farmer regularly removes, or culls, hens that have stopped laying, because they keep eating feed without giving eggs. Since feed makes up the largest share of the cost of producing an egg, every gram that is wasted cuts into the farmer's profit.
