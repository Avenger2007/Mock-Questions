# CUET Agriculture Test 12 — even key 12/13/12/13. Passage: L3 (processing market milk).

%P L3
Milk produced on millions of small farms reaches city consumers through dairy cooperatives and processing plants. Because bacteria multiply rapidly in warm milk, it should be cooled to about 4 °C within a few hours of milking, and many village societies now have bulk milk coolers for this purpose. At the reception dock of a dairy plant, every can is checked by quick platform tests. The milk is smelled and looked at (organoleptic test), and in the clot-on-boiling (COB) test a small sample is boiled: milk that curdles is too sour to be processed. The methylene blue reduction test (MBRT) estimates bacterial quality. Methylene blue dye is added to the milk and kept warm, and the faster the blue colour disappears, the more bacteria the milk contains; milk that loses its colour within about 30 minutes is graded very poor. Farmers are paid on the basis of the fat and solids-not-fat (SNF) in their milk. In the plant, the milk is clarified, standardised, pasteurised and homogenised. Homogenisation forces milk through tiny openings at high pressure, breaking fat globules into very small ones so that a cream layer does not form on standing. The milk is then packed in sealed pouches and kept cold until it reaches consumers. For simple calculations, 1 litre of milk may be taken as about 1 kg.
