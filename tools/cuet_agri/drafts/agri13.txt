# CUET Agriculture Test 13 — odd key 13/12/13/12. Passage: C4 (Phalaris minor and herbicide resistance).

%P C4
*Phalaris minor*, called littleseed canarygrass or 'gulli danda', is the most serious weed of wheat in the rice–wheat belt of Punjab and Haryana. At the seedling stage it looks so much like wheat that it is hard to remove by hand; a reddish-purple base on the stem and the absence of auricles help to tell it apart. One plant can shed hundreds of seeds, and heavy infestations have cut wheat yields by 30–40 percent or more. For many years farmers controlled it with a single herbicide, isoproturon. By the early 1990s, *Phalaris* populations that survived isoproturon had appeared, and in some fields the weed could no longer be controlled at all. The cause was the repeated use of the same herbicide year after year, which allowed rare resistant plants to multiply. Scientists then recommended new herbicides with different modes of action, such as clodinafop and sulfosulfuron, and advised rotating them rather than relying on any one. They also promoted non-chemical practices. Zero-till sowing of wheat disturbs the soil less, so fewer buried *Phalaris* seeds are brought up to germinate. Timely sowing in early November, a higher seed rate and narrower rows let the crop cover the ground before the weed. Combining such methods is called integrated weed management.
