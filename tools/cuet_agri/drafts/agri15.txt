# CUET Agriculture Test 15 — odd key 13/12/13/12. Passage: C1 (conservation and precision agriculture).

%P C1
Conservation agriculture and precision agriculture both aim to grow more with fewer resources. Conservation agriculture disturbs the soil as little as possible, keeps it covered with crop residues and diversifies the crops grown. In north-west India, burning rice straw after harvest has been common because it clears the field quickly for wheat, but the smoke pollutes the air over a large region, and the soil loses organic matter and useful organisms. Keeping the residue on the surface instead protects the soil from erosion, conserves moisture and slowly adds organic matter. Precision agriculture treats each part of a field according to its needs. Laser land levellers give a smooth, uniformly sloped field so that water spreads evenly; levelled fields need about 25 percent less irrigation water and give more even crop stands. Grid soil sampling and satellite or drone images are used to prepare maps showing zones of low and high fertility. Variable-rate applicators then put more fertilizer only where the map shows it is needed, and less elsewhere. Drones are also used to spray pesticides and nutrients over small areas quickly, and soil-moisture sensors can tell farmers when to irrigate. These technologies cost money, so custom-hiring centres and farmer groups help small farmers use them.
