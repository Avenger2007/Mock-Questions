# CUET Agriculture Test 1 — odd key 13/12/13/12. Passage: C5 (growing rice).

%P C5
Rice is the staple food of more than half of India's people, and most of it is grown in the kharif season. In irrigated areas the crop is usually raised first in a nursery. When the seedlings are about three to four weeks old, they are pulled out and transplanted into puddled fields. Puddling means ploughing the field while it is under standing water. It breaks down the soil structure, cuts the loss of water by deep percolation and buries many weeds. Two or three seedlings are planted on each hill at a spacing of about 20 cm × 15 cm. A shallow layer of water, about 5 cm deep, is kept in the field for most of the season, because rice tolerates flooding and standing water keeps weeds down. The crop suffers most from water stress at panicle initiation and at flowering. Too much nitrogen makes the plants soft and leafy, so they lodge easily and attract the brown planthopper, an insect that sucks sap at the base of the plants and causes patches of dried crop called 'hopper burn'. In the System of Rice Intensification (SRI), farmers transplant single young seedlings only 8 to 12 days old at a wider spacing of 25 cm × 25 cm and keep the soil moist but not flooded, which saves a great deal of water. The crop is harvested when about four-fifths of the grains in the panicle have turned straw-coloured.
