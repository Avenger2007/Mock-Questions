Before the monsoon, the district health officer, Dr Meena, wants villagers in Kolar block to prevent dengue. She sends an SMS in English to every registered phone: "Eliminate stagnant water to prevent vector-borne disease." She also hires a van that drives through the villages playing a recorded announcement. The van passes during the village fair, when loudspeakers are playing film songs.

Three weeks later, dengue cases are rising. No one has called the helpline number in the SMS, and the health office has no idea how people understood the message.

Dr Meena changes her plan. ASHA workers, who live in the villages, meet women's self-help groups and speak in the local language. They show pictures of a water tank, a flower pot and an old tyre holding water, and ask the women where such water collects in their own homes. When the women say that covering tanks is hard, the workers show them how to use a cloth. Within a month, the number of new cases falls.
