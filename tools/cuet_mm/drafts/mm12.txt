When several factories in an industrial town shut down suddenly, thousands of migrant workers begin walking to their villages, hundreds of kilometres away. Meera, a photojournalist, travels with one group for four days.

Her paper publishes twelve of her pictures over two pages as a single story of the journey. It opens with a wide shot of a long line of walkers on an empty highway, followed by close-ups of blistered feet, a mother carrying a sleeping child and a man sharing his last roti. Under each picture is a short text giving the person's name (used with their permission), the place and what is happening. For the picture of the child, Meera chose a frame in which the child's face cannot be seen.

While laying out the page, an editor suggests cropping a police van out of one picture "to make it look cleaner". Meera refuses, saying the van, which was stopping walkers at a district border, is part of the story. None of the pictures was posed.
