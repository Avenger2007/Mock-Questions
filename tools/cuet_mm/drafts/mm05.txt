Manthan ("The Churning", 1976) was directed by Shyam Benegal. It tells the story of a veterinary doctor who comes to a Gujarat village to set up a milk cooperative. He is opposed by the private dairy owner, who buys milk cheaply, and by divisions of caste within the village. In the end, the villagers begin to run the cooperative themselves.

The film drew on the cooperative dairy movement that Verghese Kurien had built in Gujarat. It was paid for in an unusual way: about five lakh member farmers of the Gujarat milk cooperatives each gave two rupees, so the farmers were the film's producers. The film was shot on location in a village, with a small budget and actors such as Smita Patil, Girish Karnad and Naseeruddin Shah, who came from theatre and the film institute rather than from commercial stardom. It won the National Film Award for the best Hindi feature film. The cooperatives later took the film to villages to show farmers what a cooperative could do.
