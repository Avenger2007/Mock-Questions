For years, trucks had been dumping construction debris into Kalyani Lake on the edge of a small town. One evening a college student posted photos of the shrinking lake with the hashtag #SaveKalyaniLake. Within days, a short video of dead fish on the shore had been shared thousands of times, and many residents changed their profile pictures to a drawing of the lake. An online petition to the municipal corporation gathered 40,000 signatures. Through WhatsApp groups, volunteers organised a weekend clean-up that drew 600 people.

At the height of the campaign, a message began circulating that claimed the lake would be "sold to a builder tomorrow". It carried an image of an official-looking government order. Before reacting, the admins of the main groups checked with the municipal office and a fact-checking website. They found that the order had been doctored, and posted a correction asking members not to forward the message.

A month later, the corporation announced fencing around the lake and an inquiry into the dumping. Some critics still described the online support as "slacktivism", saying that liking a post costs nothing.
