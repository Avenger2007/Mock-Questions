Radio in India began with amateurs. In June 1923 the Radio Club of Bombay made the first broadcast, and similar clubs followed in other cities. In 1927 a private company, the Indian Broadcasting Company, set up stations in Bombay and Calcutta. It ran into heavy losses and went into liquidation, and in 1930 the government took over broadcasting as the Indian State Broadcasting Service. In 1936 the service was renamed All India Radio.

After Independence, AIR expanded fast. From 1957 it came to be known as Akashvani. That year many Indian listeners were tuning in to the film-song programmes of Radio Ceylon, so AIR started Vividh Bharati, a service of light music and entertainment. For decades broadcasting remained a government monopoly. Then, in 2001, private companies began running FM stations in Indian cities, and from 2004 colleges and community groups were allowed to run community radio.
