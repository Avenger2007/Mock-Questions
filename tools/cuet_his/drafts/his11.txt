The most imposing structure in the area of Vijayanagara known as the "king's palace" is a massive platform, now called the "Mahanavami dibba". It rises from a base of about 11,000 square feet to a height of about 40 feet, and there is evidence that it once supported a wooden structure. The base of the platform is covered with carved reliefs.

The platform is linked with the Mahanavami, literally the "great ninth day", a Hindu festival held during the autumn months of September and October. During this festival the rulers of Vijayanagara displayed their prestige, power and suzerainty. The ceremonies included the worship of an image, the worship of the state horse, and the sacrifice of buffaloes and other animals. There were dances, wrestling matches, and processions of caparisoned horses, elephants, chariots and soldiers. Nayakas and subordinate kings made ritual presentations before the king and his guests.

On the last day of the festival, the king inspected his own army and the armies of the nayakas in a grand ceremony held in an open field. On this occasion the nayakas brought rich gifts for the king, as well as the tribute they owed. Visitors such as Domingo Paes, a Portuguese traveller, left vivid descriptions of these celebrations.
