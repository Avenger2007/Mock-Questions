On 12 May 1875, ryots from the surrounding countryside gathered at Supa, a large village in Poona district, and attacked the shops and houses of the sahukars, the moneylenders. They demanded the moneylenders' account books and debt bonds, and set them on fire. They also looted grain shops and, in some cases, burnt down the houses of sahukars.

From Poona the revolt spread to Ahmednagar, and within weeks to many more villages over a wide area. Everywhere the pattern was the same: sahukars were attacked, their account books burnt and their debt bonds destroyed. Frightened moneylenders fled their villages, often leaving their property behind. The British set up police posts in the villages to restore order, and many ryots were arrested.

The Government of Bombay, alarmed by the spread of the revolt, set up a commission of enquiry. The Deccan Riots Commission held enquiries in the affected districts, recorded statements from ryots, sahukars and eyewitnesses, and compiled statistics on revenue rates, prices and interest. Its report was presented to the British Parliament in 1878. The government wanted to know whether its revenue demand had caused the revolt, and the Commission concluded that the moneylenders, not the revenue demand, were to blame. Historians therefore read such official reports with care, keeping in mind the concerns of those who produced them.
