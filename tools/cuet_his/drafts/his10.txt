Early in 1930 Gandhi announced that he would lead a march to break the salt law. This law gave the state a monopoly over the making and selling of salt. Salt was used in every household, yet Indians could not make it even for their own use and had to buy it at a high price. By choosing salt, Gandhi turned a grievance shared by everyone into a symbol of unjust rule.

On 12 March 1930 he set out from his ashram at Sabarmati, walking towards the sea. He had told the Viceroy, Lord Irwin, of his plans in advance, but Irwin failed to grasp their importance. Large crowds gathered along the way. In village after village Gandhi urged upper-caste officials to resign their posts, and asked people to take up spinning and give up liquor.

About three weeks later he reached Dandi, on the coast, and made a fistful of salt, breaking the law. Parallel salt marches were held in other parts of the country, and thousands were arrested. The march was followed closely by newspapers in Europe and America. An American news magazine that had first mocked his thin frame soon began to describe him as a saint, and the march made Gandhi known across the world.
