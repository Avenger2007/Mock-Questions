%P H1
When excavators cleared the Lower Town of Mohenjodaro, they found that the settlement had been planned before it was built. Streets and lanes were laid out on an approximate grid, crossing at right angles, and the drains were put down first; the houses were then built along them. Every house had to have at least one wall along a street so that its waste water could flow into the street drain.

The street drains were made of bricks set in mortar and were covered with loose bricks that could be lifted for cleaning. Long channels had sumps at intervals where waste collected and could be cleared out. Bathrooms inside houses were paved with bricks and their drains ran through the outer wall. One of the excavators, Ernest Mackay, judged it the most complete ancient system of drainage then known.

The bricks themselves, whether sun-dried or baked, followed a standard ratio: the length was four times the height and the breadth twice the height. Houses had no windows in the walls along the ground level, and the main entrance did not give a direct view of the interior or the courtyard. On the western side stood the Citadel, smaller but higher than the Lower Town, built on mud-brick platforms and walled.
