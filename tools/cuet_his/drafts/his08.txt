The Prayaga prashasti was composed in Sanskrit by Harisena, a court poet who also held high office under Samudragupta. It was inscribed on an Ashokan pillar at Allahabad, so an old pillar came to carry the praise of a much later king.

Harisena presents his master as a ruler without equal. The kings of Aryavarta, in the north, were uprooted and their lands taken over. The rulers of Dakshinapatha, in the south, were captured and then, by the king's grace, released. Frontier states and forest peoples paid tribute and came to the court, while distant rulers, among them the Shakas and the ruler of Sri Lanka, sought his favour. Samudragupta is compared to the gods and praised as a poet and a skilled musician.

Historians value such texts but read them with care. A prashasti was composed by a poet at the court to praise his patron, so it is silent about defeats and tends to magnify victories. Its claims have to be checked against other evidence, such as coins and other inscriptions.
