The Santhals began coming into Bengal around the 1780s. Zamindars hired them to reclaim land and extend cultivation, and British officials soon saw them as ideal settlers. The Paharias of the Rajmahal hills had refused to give up shifting cultivation and their forest life, but the Santhals were skilled at clearing forest and ploughing the land. They were invited to settle in the foothills.

By 1832 a large area had been marked out as the Damin-i-Koh, the land of the Santhals. They were to live within its boundaries, use the plough and become settled cultivators. The settlements grew quickly. The number of Santhal villages rose from 40 in 1838 to 1,473 by 1851, and their population went up from about 3,000 to over 82,000. As cultivation spread, the Paharias were pushed deeper into the hills, on to drier and rockier land.

The Santhals soon found that the land they had cleared was slipping out of their hands. The state levied heavy taxes, moneylenders charged high rates of interest and took over land when debts went unpaid, and zamindars asserted control over the area. In 1855–56 the Santhals rose in revolt. After the revolt was suppressed, the British carved out a separate district, the Santhal Pargana, and laid down special laws for it.
