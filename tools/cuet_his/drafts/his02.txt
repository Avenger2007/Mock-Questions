Abu'l Fazl's *Ain-i Akbari* explains how the Mughal state under Akbar assessed land for revenue. Land was first classified by how regularly it was cultivated. Polaj was land cultivated every year for each crop and never allowed to lie fallow. Parauti was land left out of cultivation for a time so that it could recover its strength. Chachar was land that had lain fallow for three or four years, and banjar was land left uncultivated for five years or more.

For polaj and parauti, each of the good, middling and bad kinds was measured, and the produce of the three was added together. One-third of this average produce was taken as the state's demand. Chachar and banjar lands were brought back into cultivation on lighter terms, with the demand rising year by year.

The *Ain* also separated the amount assessed (jama) from the amount actually collected (hasil). Historians value the text for its range of detail, but they also note its limits. Its totals do not always add up, its data are uneven from province to province, and its lists of prices and wages come mostly from the region around Agra, the capital.
