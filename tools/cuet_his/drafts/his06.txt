Ibn Battuta was struck by how fast news travelled in the Delhi Sultanate. He described two kinds of postal system. The horse post, called *uluq*, used royal horses stationed at intervals of about four miles. The foot post, called *dawa*, had three stations to every mile, and at each one runners waited in readiness.

A runner of the foot post carried a rod about two cubits long with copper bells fixed at its top. When he set out from a station, he held the letter in one hand and the rod in the other and ran at full speed. The next runner heard the bells, made himself ready, took the letter and ran on to the following station.

Ibn Battuta thought the foot post was quicker than the horse post. It was even used to bring fruits from Khurasan, which were much prized in India. He noted that a march from Sind to Delhi took about fifty days, while the reports of the sultan's informers covered the same distance in about five. Merchants, too, could use the system to send information and to remit credit across long distances.
