# Coverage map — CUET Mass Media, Tests 1–15

This map was written **before** any test, so that repeats are prevented rather than fixed. Rules:

- Every case set in §A is used **once** in the series.
- Each **answer fact** is a short key such as `lasswell model 1948`, `alam ara 1931` or `rti act 2005`.
  - An answer fact is the fact a question turns on. Each correct pair in a match item, and each step in a sequence, is its own fact.
  - A key may be used **at most twice in the series**, in different tests and different formats.
  - Before a test ships, its keys are checked against the ledgers in §C (`mm_check.py`, flag `REPEAT-TARGET`) and then added to them (`mark_mm.py`).
  - Distractors may repeat.
- Every test covers all eight units in the quotas set in the blueprint (§3).

## B. Slot rotation (keeps each unit's questions moving across the series)

- **M1:** levels (intra-, inter-, group, mass); models (Aristotle, Lasswell, Shannon–Weaver, Osgood–Schramm, Berlo SMCR, Schramm's field of experience, Dance helical, Westley–MacLean); barriers and noise; feedback; culture (definition, transmission, popular culture); representation and stereotyping; social change (development communication, SITE, campaigns, movements); newspapers (Hicky 1780, *Samachar Darpan*, *Udant Martand*, nationalist press, Vernacular Press Act 1878, Emergency censorship 1975); radio (1923–1957, Vividh Bharati, FM, community radio); TV (1959, Krishi Darshan, SITE, Doordarshan, colour 1982, *Hum Log*, *Buniyaad*, *Ramayan*, cable 1991, Prasar Bharati); McLuhan.
- **M2:** definition of news; news values (timeliness, proximity, prominence, impact, conflict, human interest, novelty); components (headline, byline, dateline, lead, body); journalist's qualities (nose for news, inquisitiveness, language, trustworthiness, empathy); inverted pyramid; 5Ws and 1H; headline types (banner, deck, kicker, jump head, boxed); copy editing and marks; photojournalism (captions, photo essay, visual depiction); ethics (sensationalism, fake news, paid news, plagiarism, advertorials, partisan reporting, sting operations); law (19(1)(a), 19(2), defamation, contempt, Official Secrets Act, RTI, Press Council, Cinematograph Act, IT Act, Copyright Act, judgments).
- **M3:** advertising concept and process (agency, client, brief, creative, media planning); functions; types (product, institutional, public service, cooperative, informational, comparative, cross promotion, merchandising, covert / product placement, surrogate); forms (print, broadcast, outdoor, transit, online); campaigns (Amul, Jago Grahak Jago, Incredible India, Pulse Polio); ASCI; PR (Ivy Lee, Bernays, PRSI, RACE); PR tools (press release, conference, house journal, events, sponsorship); brand image; crisis management; the PRO.
- **M4:** pre-production (concept, script, storyboard, budget, recce, casting, crew); production (shoot, cameras, lights, sound, floor manager); post-production (editing, dubbing, graphics, music, mixing); programme formats (news, talk, reality, game, soap, docudrama, chat); scheduling (stripping, stacking, hammocking, tent-poling); audiences and ratings (TRP, BARC); shots and lenses.
- **M5:** script traits (conversational language, active voice, simple sentences, no jargon, imagery); writing for the ear; microphones (dynamic, condenser, ribbon; omni, cardioid, bidirectional); amplifier, mixer, speakers; studio (acoustics, on-air light, talkback); transmitter; AM/FM; recording and editing; formats (talk, feature, drama, phone-in).
- **M6:** Lumière brothers (Cinématographe, 1895, *Arrival of a Train*); Flaherty (*Nanook of the North*, 1922; *Moana*); Grierson (coined "documentary", 1926; *Drifters*); Phalke (*Raja Harishchandra*, 1913); film stages (pre-, production, post-, distribution, exhibition); documentary making; genre theory and genres; parallel cinema (*Bhuvan Shome*, *Ankur*, *Manthan*, FFC/NFDC, Ray, Benegal).
- **M7:** definition; platform types (networking, microblogging, blogging, photo and video sharing, messaging, professional); democracy (voice, accountability, misinformation); collective identities; cyber activism (Arab Spring, #MeToo, Nirbhaya protests, India Against Corruption); cyber crime (bullying, stalking, trolling, phishing, identity theft, fraud; cybercrime.gov.in, helpline 1930, IT Act 2000); netiquette (identify yourself, respect privacy, appropriate language and images, no spam).
- **M8:** the internet as the meeting point of media; the traditional model (few to many, gigantic organisations, huge infrastructure, large revenue); the changed paradigm (individuals post, one medium triggers another, many sources, global and rapid distribution, opinion formed quickly); socio-political implications and democracy; emerging trends (OTT, podcasts, mobile journalism, AI, data journalism); convergence (technological, economic, cultural); citizen journalism; Web 2.0.
