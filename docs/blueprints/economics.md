# Blueprint — CUET (UG) 2027 Economics (309)

**Syllabus:** `CUET SYLLABUS/md/Economics - 309.md` (NTA "for the year 2026", latest published). Three courses: Introductory Microeconomics (4 units), Introductory Macroeconomics (5 units) and Indian Economic Development (4 units). **Statistics for Economics is not in the syllabus** and is never tested.
**Series:** 15 tests, `CUET/Economics/CUET ECONOMICS TEST 1–15.json`.

## 1. Paper pattern (NTA)

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Questions | 50, all compulsory (no internal choice) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Marking | +5 / −1 / 0 |
| Options | 4, single correct |

## 2. PYQ analysis (CUET 2025 Economics — analysed, not stored)

Source: a scanned copy of a 2025 Economics / Business Economics paper published by [AglaSem](https://docs.aglasem.com/org/nta/cuet/question-paper?subject=economics) (bilingual, about 40 pages). A sample of its pages was read; no question was copied.

- **Formats seen:** direct MCQs ("The point on the supply curve at which a firm earns only normal profit is called …"), **Match List-I/List-II** (utility concepts; national income aggregates such as GDP at market price, NDP at factor cost, GVA at factor cost), **multi-statement questions** ("Which conditions must hold for a firm to maximise profit?"), **arrange-in-order** (the chain of events after a leftward shift of demand), and a **passage set** at the end (GST — destination-based tax, input tax credit, cascading).
- **Content spread:** all three courses appear — consumer behaviour, firm under perfect competition, national income aggregates and price indices, money and banking, excess and deficient demand, balance of trade/payments, and Indian economic development topics.
- **Difficulty:** mostly easy to moderate; difficulty comes from close options (e.g. balance of trade vs balance of payments), the logic of graphs described in words, and multi-statement judgement rather than long calculations.

**What this means for OurMocks:** precise NCERT definitions, chains of cause and effect (shifts, multiplier, money creation), short numericals that need no calculator, and close distractors — never content beyond the syllabus.

## 4. Difficulty (every test)

**8 easy / 32 medium / 10 hard.** Rough split: micro 3/10/3, macro 3/12/4, IED 2/10/3 (±1).

- **Easy:** one NCERT definition, fact or classification.
- **Medium:** a one-step numerical, a cause-and-effect link, a close distractor set, a table reading, or matching.
- **Hard:** a two-step numerical (e.g. national income by two methods, multiplier with a change in investment, elasticity by two methods), a chain of events, or a multi-statement judgement.

## 6. Key and order

- Key per test 13 / 12 / 13 / 12 (rotate); no letter more than 3 times in a row; the correct option is never clearly the longest.
- Statement and assertion–reason answers are varied (not always "(A), (B) and (C) only" or "A").
- `shuffle_test.py` gives random exam order and keeps the case set together.

## 7. Numbers and facts

- All numericals are solvable without a calculator.
- Indian-economy facts follow NCERT *Indian Economic Development* (rationalised edition). Where a figure changes over time (e.g. GDP growth, workforce shares), questions use NCERT's own table values and say so, or test the trend rather than the exact number.

## 8. Explanations

- Numericals and data questions: `Answer:` → `Given` → `Step 1…n` → `Common mistake`.
- Concept questions: `Answer:` → `Why` → `Why not the others` → `Key point`.
- Every explanation follows NCERT Class 12 *Introductory Microeconomics*, *Introductory Macroeconomics* and *Indian Economic Development*.

## 9. Out of syllabus — never test

Statistics for Economics (Class 11: collection and presentation of data, central tendency, dispersion, correlation, index-number construction), non-competitive markets (monopoly, oligopoly — removed from the rationalised Microeconomics textbook), the IS–LM model and anything beyond NCERT's two-sector model, and the poverty and infrastructure chapters removed from the rationalised IED textbook. Indifference curves, MRS and the consumer's optimum are tested only as NCERT presents them in Chapter 2.
